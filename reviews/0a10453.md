## New findings

### D1 (medium, CONFIRMED) — MirrorBC still does not converge along a wall at N = 160, and its error grows in time; the remaining case is not explained by a gap
The channel test (`half_diag2.jl`, `half_diag3.jl`, unjittered, MUSCL3, R = 2.5Δx) measures the max interior error
at t = 0.25/0.5/1/2 for N = 40/80/160:

| wall offset | condition | N = 80, t = 1 / 2 | N = 160, t = 1 / 2 |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 0.1 h (snapped) | exact ghosts | 0.013 / 0.021 | 0.0064 / 0.0065 |
| 0.1 h (snapped) | Mirror2 | 0.025 / 0.035 | **0.018 / 0.026** |
| 0.0 (lattice wall) | Mirror2 | 0.038 / 0.056 | **0.029 / 0.044** |
| 0.25 h (snapped) | Mirror2 | 0.016 / 0.024 | 0.010 / 0.013 |
| 0.5 h (not snapped) | exact ghosts | 0.015 / 0.019 | 0.0077 / 0.0080 |
| 0.5 h (not snapped) | Mirror2 | 0.125 / 0.207 | **0.106 / 0.179** |
| 0.75 h (not snapped) | Mirror2 | 0.057 / 0.090 | **0.046 / 0.072** |

Exact ghosts converge at first order and stay flat in time. With `MirrorBC` the error grows roughly linearly in time
at every offset, and from N = 80 to 160 the EOC is 0.4–0.5 (0.1 h, lattice wall) and 0.2–0.3 (0.5 h, 0.75 h).
Bounded and unbounded mirrors give identical results (`half_diag.out`). The max error sits on the first interior
row. The static ghost error (exact interior data, ghosts compared with u at the mirror point) is tiny and
converges at 4th order: 2.9e-3/2.2e-4/1.5e-5 at 0.5 h. So the ghost fit is accurate. What goes wrong is the
feedback of the mirrored numerical solution into the near-wall stencils.

This undermines two claims of the commit. First, the docs (Stability item 0) say "the mirror converges with about
the error of exact ghost values". That holds at N ≤ 80 and t ≤ 1 only. Second, the docs say the 0.5 h case is "the
largest gap the snapping leaves" and converges at "roughly first order (0.33/0.14/0.13)". 0.14 → 0.13 is EOC 0.1,
and at 0.5 h the mirror points of the first ghost row fall exactly onto the first interior row, so there is no gap
to extrapolate across. The lattice wall (offset 0, the case the earlier fixes treated as solved) shows the same
growth, so most of this predates the commit. The snapping improves the offsets below h/2 and does not touch the
rest.

On the disc (acoustics Bessel mode, `disc_ac.out`), MirrorBC and exact ghosts are within 10 % of each other on both
trees. That test is dominated by the interior dissipation, so it does not show the problem.

- Fix (to investigate): run the channel to N = 160 and t = 2, and look at the near-wall stencils (one-sided weights
  of the first row against the mirrored ghosts; the even reflection as an eigenvalue problem of the semi-discrete
  operator on a 1-D cross-section). Correct Stability item 0 and the "slowest case" sentence to what the data show.
  Consider an N = 160 or longer-time check in the suite, or at least one that would catch error growth in time
  (e.g. `e(t=1) < 1.5 e(t=0.25)` at N = 80, which currently gives 0.0245/0.0139 = 1.76).

### D2 (medium, CONFIRMED) — on jittered clouds a snapped point can land almost on an interior point: spacing collapses
`Domains.jl:_snap_near_wall!`. The clash test only compares snapped points with *each other* (`taken`). It does not
compare them with the interior points: the jittered interior points near the wall, and the points that
`_reflect_inside` put back inside, which sit up to the jitter distance inside the wall. The triangle-inequality
bound (lattice distance h minus the snap distance h/2) holds only on an unjittered lattice. With jitter j·h per
axis, two lattice neighbours can come within (1 − 2j)·h before the snap moves one of them by up to h/2.

`spacing*.out`, minimum interior spacing / h, 23964b6 → 0a10453:

| grid | j = 0.2 | j = 0.3 | j = 0.45 |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| disc r = 1, h = 0.037 | 0.61 → 0.34 | 0.42 → **0.19** | 0.12 → 0.10 |
| union of two discs, h = 0.04 | 0.64 → 0.29 | 0.47 → **0.19** | 0.22 → **0.005** |
| box minus disc | 0.61 → 0.44 | 0.42 → 0.21 | 0.13 → 0.13 |
| 3-D box 1.03 × 1.01 × 0.98, h = 0.1 | – | 0.45 → **0.085** (F32 0.12) | – |
| 3-D ball, h = 0.1 | – | 0.42 → 0.21 (F32 0.16) | – |
| wedge 30°, `jit_cmp` (j = 0.4) | | | 0.25 → **0.056** |

The 0.005 h pair in the union is a snapped point (φ = 0) and a reflected point (φ = −0.0027 h). Consequences
measured: the CFL time step of MUSCL3 drops by up to 19 % (union, j = 0.3: dt/h 0.0915 → 0.0737). Unjittered
grids lose 5–13 % from the 0.55 h spacing alone (disc 0.139 → 0.133, box minus disc 0.139 → 0.126, union 0.139 →
0.121), which is inherent to snapping. The default `ParticleAdaptivity` (`min_gap = 0.5`) treats these pairs as
merge candidates (D3). The docs say "on a lattice a snapped point stays at least Δx/2 from every other lattice
point". That is correct, but it says nothing about jittered clouds, and the new spacing test (`:996`) is skipped for
`jit > 0`.

- Fix: put the interior points within ~h of a wall into the clash hash as well (all `inner_points` with
  `signed_distance > -h`, or every inner point; the hash is cheap). On a clash with an interior point within h/4
  (or (1 − 2j)h), either drop the snapped point (the interior point is already at the wall) or push the outside
  point out to h/2 as now. Add a jittered minimum-spacing check (e.g. ≥ 0.25 h at j = 0.3 on the disc).

### D3 (low, CONFIRMED) — adaptivity can move snapped wall particles off the wall; it never applies the h/2 rule
`Adaptivity.jl:355-395`. Insertions and merges use pairs of interior particles only, so adaptivity never creates a
ghost and the h/2 ghost rule is not at risk. That is the right design: inserted particles are interior, and ghosts
are generated once. Merges, however, replace a close pair by its midpoint. On the snapped jittered clouds of D2,
the first `adapt_particles!` call merged 2–4 pairs, and the wall lost 1–2 of its on-wall particles (disc j = 0.3:
54 → 52, box 1.03 × 1.01 j = 0.2: 15 → 14; `adapt_d.out`; 0 on 23964b6 and on unjittered grids). Small. It goes
away with the D2 fix. A merge partner on the wall could also keep the wall position instead of the midpoint.

### D4 (low, CONFIRMED by reading) — the clash hash is not periodic, and the comment names reflex corners only
`key(p) = floor.(p / cell)` uses wrapped coordinates, so two snapped points on either side of a periodic seam
(x ≈ min and x ≈ max) are never compared. That needs a reflex corner at the seam, which is rare. The docstring and
docs say clashes happen "only at a reflex corner". They can also happen behind a convex corner, where every point in
the normal cone projects onto the corner point. I saw no clash-induced spacing problem in the unjittered probes
(minimum 0.547 h), so this is wording.

- Fix: wrap the key on periodic axes (or compare wrapped differences), and say "at corners".

## Correctness checks that passed

- **Unaffected grids**: all 1632 rectangular probe grids, unjittered and jittered (j ≤ 0.45), F32/F64, 1–3-D,
  periodic and anisotropic, are bitwise identical to 23964b6. An outside lattice point of a lattice-aligned box is
  at least (1 − j)h > h/2 from the wall, so it never enters `near_wall`. The step with lattice walls and the legacy
  disc are unchanged (`jit_cmp`). Spherical grids, off-lattice boxes, CSG obstacles and the wedge change, as
  documented.
- **Snapped points are interior and on the wall**: every interior particle has φ ≤ 0 in every probe (also the suite's
  check), and the rounding fallback nudges by 64 eps. Behind convex corners and in the reflex corner of the step the
  snapped points stay ≥ 0.547 h apart without jitter. The push-out puts the ghost at exactly φ = h/2 along the normal
  of the same nearest point (convex outside regions). Minimum ghost φ is ≥ 0.500 h in all 60+ probe grids,
  F32 included.
- **Lower-bound filter**: `signed_distance < h/2` is a lower bound for intersections/differences and exact for
  primitives and unions, so no candidate is missed. Candidates whose true φ ≥ h/2 go back to the ghosts.
- **Cost**: grid construction old → new (median of 3, `-t 2`): 2-D box 400² 0.101 → 0.109 s, disc 400² 0.086 →
  0.070 s, box minus disc 0.050 → 0.044 s, rotated plate 0.054 → 0.046 s, 3-D box 64³ 0.481 → 0.484 s, 3-D ball 64³
  0.273 → 0.291 s, 3-D box minus ball 0.258 → 0.271 s. `get_points` alone is ≤ 0.03 s everywhere. No measurable
  cost.
- **Volumes**: Shepard volumes on the new grids are in the same range (interior V/h² 0.67–0.73 vs 0.71–0.76
  unjittered disc). The larger-box volume is analytic (π). The renormalisation is unchanged.
- **N3**: the cap now comes from the fitted donor; a lower degree needs less support, so the moved donor stays
  valid.

## Verdict

The commit does what it set out to do for the geometry. Every ghost is now at least h/2 outside the wall on
shape-based domains, unjittered and jittered, 1–3-D, F32 included. The interior reaches curved walls, obstacles and
off-lattice box faces. Lattice-aligned boxes are bitwise unchanged, jittered or not, and the cost is nil. The static
mirror fit now converges at order 2–3 on discs, balls, obstacles and the step, where it stalled before. The channel
with a wall 0.02–0.45 h off the lattice converges up to N = 80. N2–N4 are resolved, and the suite passes.

Two things remain. First, `MirrorBC` along a wall still loses convergence at N = 160 and its error grows in time,
on every wall including lattice walls, and badly at offsets of 0.45–0.75 h (D1). The cause is not the ghost fit,
whose static error is O(h⁴), and the docs' explanation of the remaining case does not hold. Second, on jittered
clouds the snapped points are not kept away from the interior points, so the minimum spacing can collapse to 0.005 h
and the time step shrinks (D2).
