## Findings

### N1 (high, CONFIRMED) — MirrorBC still fails at walls that are not on the lattice, jittered or not: B-F1 is fixed only for box faces
`src/Grid/Domains.jl:270-286` (`get_points`) and the mirror fit (`BoundaryConditions.jl`). The reflection only
handles **interior** lattice points that the jitter carries outside. A wall that passes just above a lattice row
still makes that row's points ghosts that sit almost on the wall. On a lattice-aligned box face this cannot
happen, because the wall row is snapped onto the wall. On every other wall it can: curved walls, rotated or offset
obstacles, steps whose edge is not a multiple of h, and the upper wall of a box whose extent is not a multiple of h.

`jit_cmp.out`: minimum ghost wall distance 0.019 h (disc), 0.0027 h (3-D ball), 0.11 h (30° wedge), 0.08 h
(rotated plate), unchanged by the fix.

More importantly, the accuracy failure does not need jitter. Unlike the lattice-wall case, the mirror point of a
ghost at φ < h lies in the gap between the wall and the first interior row, so the fit extrapolates.
`offwall0.jl` and `offwall.jl`: the commit's channel (MUSCL3, RK3-SSP, R = 2.5Δx) with the bottom wall moved to
y = δ by `Cuboid((0,0),(1,1); open = (true,false)) ∩ HalfSpace((0, δ), (0, −1))`, Gaussian centred on the wall.
Max interior error at N = 20/40/80:

| δ, jitter, t | exact ghosts | OutflowBC | MirrorBC(degree 2) |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 0.1h, 0, 0.25 | 0.056 / 0.026 / 0.014 | 0.18 / 0.063 / 0.041 | **0.23 / 0.28 / 0.33** |
| 0.1h, 0, 1.0 | 0.091 / 0.036 / 0.016 | 0.38 / 0.16 / 0.091 | **0.41 / 0.67 / 0.63** |
| 0.02h, 0, 1.0 | 0.092 / 0.034 / 0.016 | 0.38 / 0.17 / 0.092 | **0.38 / 0.67 / 0.61** |
| 0.1h, 0.2, 0.25 | 0.080 / 0.055 / 0.028 | 0.22 / 0.39 / 0.24 | **0.22 / 0.22 / 0.27** |
| 0.1h, 0.2, 1.0 | 0.10 / 0.062 / 0.023 | 0.44 / 0.33 / 0.31 | **0.36 / 0.58 / 0.66** |
| 0.5h, 0.2, 0.25 | 0.094 / 0.034 / 0.016 | 0.21 / 0.097 / 0.042 | 0.17 / 0.072 / 0.049 |

So MirrorBC, the documented wall condition, does not converge and grows to O(1) when the wall lies within about
0.1 h outside a lattice row, even on an unjittered lattice. It converges only slowly when the wall lies 0.5 h
from a row, and it is worse than OutflowBC in most of these rows. On a curved wall every offset occurs somewhere
along the wall. The commit message ("MirrorBC then converges at the error of exact ghost values") and Stability
item 0 of `boundary_conditions.md` say the cause was the jitter of the wall row. The data say the cause is ghosts
close to the wall, whose mirror points fall between the wall and the first interior row. The jitter of a lattice
wall row is only one way to get them.

- Fix (to investigate):
  - (a) In the point generator, keep the *whole* near-wall band away from the wall: push ghost candidates with
    φ < c·h (c ≈ 0.5) out along the normal to φ = c·h, or snap the nearest outside row onto the wall and classify
    it as interior, as box faces do now. Check the collisions this creates with the next row.
  - (b) In the condition, give ghosts with φ < c·h a value that needs no extrapolation, e.g. the fit at the wall
    point. This is review B's option (b).
  - Either way, add the off-lattice channel (δ = 0.1h, unjittered, N = 40/80) as a convergence test. A disc test
    would also do.

### N2 (low, CONFIRMED) — `wall_geometry` is still inexact behind very acute corners (below ~13°), and slow there
`Domains.jl:349-389`. At these angles the alternating projections in `_onto_boundary` converge like cos²α:
0.992^512 ≈ 0.02 at 5°, so 512 steps leave a point off the boundary. The descent then starts from that point.
`wg2.out`, 2-D wedge `Cuboid ∩ HalfSpace`, ghosts within 0.3 of the corner, max relative error of the distance:
3°: 0.50, 5°: 0.14, 7°: 0.022, 9°: 1.8e-3, 11°: 7.5e-5, 13°: 1.7e-6, 15°: 2e-8. Each call behind such a corner
costs 120–140 µs; one call at 5.7° took 40 ms in `wg.jl`, while most points in that probe take about 0.5 µs. Such
corners are rare (thin wedges, sharp trailing edges), but `domains.md` claims "exact to round-off at any corner
angle".

- Fix: correct the docs claim (exact from about 15°), or project onto the intersection of the two active pieces
  directly when both are half spaces or planar faces (the corner point), or use Dykstra's algorithm. Add a 5° case
  to the wedge test.

### N3 (low, CONFIRMED by reading) — the MOOD order cap is now taken from the wall donor, not from the donor that is fitted
`BoundaryConditions.jl:331-352`. `o = orders[j]` is read before the loop, and `j = jd` is set afterwards. Before
this change the order was read after the donor moved. When the donor moves inwards, the degree is capped by the
order of the original wall particle, while the fit uses the moved donor's stencil. If MOOD has lowered the moved
donor (a shock one row in), its cap is ignored. The opposite case, a lowered wall donor, now caps a healthy inner
donor too. Small effect.

- Fix: also apply `min(q, orders[jb] - 1)` when a neighbour is chosen (its own order), or document which donor's
  order counts.

### N4 (low, CONFIRMED) — the crop check refuses tight boxes around shapes with conservative bounding boxes, and loose boxes are ignored without a notice
`Domains.jl:69-77`. The check compares against `bounding_box(shape)`. For `Rotated` shapes, and for
intersections, that box is conservative: `Rotated(Ball(0, 1), π/4)` has the box ±1.414. So
`mins = (-1, -1), maxs = (1, 1)`, which crops nothing, throws ArgumentError (`misc_a.jl`). A *larger* box on a
bounded axis is silently replaced by the shape's box. A config that relied on `Grid_mins`/`Grid_maxs` to anchor
the lattice gets a shifted lattice without a message (`Ball((0.5,0.5), 0.4)` with mins 0 → mins 0.1).

- Fix: for the refusal, compare against the shape (e.g. check that the box contains every boundary sample of a
  coarse `_clipped_volume` lattice) or say in the error that rotated/combined boxes are conservative. For loose
  boxes, warn once (`@warn`/`maxlog = 1`) when given `mins`/`maxs` on a bounded axis are ignored.

## Correctness checks that passed

- **Random-number order unchanged**: `jitter` draws `rand(rng, T)` once per axis in axis order, as before; the
  position arithmetic (`lattice + jitter`, then wrap) is the same. Unjittered grids are bitwise identical to
  5409209: all rectangular (1-3-D, F32/F64, periodic, anisotropic) and spherical grids of `probe_regress`.
  Jitter 0 gives `-0.0`/`0.0`, and `iszero` covers both.
- **Only reflected points move** (`jit_cmp.out`, 17 grids): moved = removed old ghosts. No retagging at unchanged
  positions. The new ghosts are just the narrow band of the new interior points. Fully periodic grids are
  unchanged. Legacy domains fall back to the lattice position (6 of 550 points in the legacy disc). Corners:
  reflection through the corner lands inside, otherwise the lattice position is used. Minimum spacing is
  unchanged (one case 2.9 % smaller at jitter 0.45, within the jitter bound). Float32 and 1-D/3-D are fine.
- **Who is affected**: every jittered grid with walls changes (792 of the 1728 probe grids). In the probe that is
  15–333 ghosts per grid becoming interior, e.g. 2-D h = 1/20: 401 → 441 interior. The docs mention it
  (Point generation step 3).
- **Volumes**: `_clipped_volume` is analytic for `Cuboid` (open axes span the box), `Ball`, and
  `Tagged`/`Translated` balls (π). Otherwise a single lattice integration (F32 CSG 0.8743373 vs 0.8743363). The
  `volume` keyword works. `domain_volume(pg) === geom.volume`. The legacy constructor stores NaN and keeps the
  label-dispatched `domain_volume`. Mach3Step (legacy 7-argument call) is unaffected. No caller uses the
  positional 8-argument form.
- **Tags**: `Tagged(…, 0/-2)` throws. A tag function returning 0 throws at grid construction. A Float return value
  is converted (2.0 → 2).
- **Wall geometry** behind 15°–170° wedges, in rotated wedges, for far points (−3…7 around a unit wedge) and in the
  step domain: exact to ≤ 7e-11. Union outside, L notch, box minus disc and Cuboid are unchanged. Termination is
  bounded (64 × 30 × 512 projections at worst; measured worst 0.4 ms except N2).
- **Ghost fit**: F64 exact for every D/degree/jitter probed. Balls exact at the mirror point with all mirror
  points inside. KA parity at round-off. 0 B per call on Polyester, KA launch bytes unchanged. Tag order is
  irrelevant (bitwise). `@noinline` returning two `SMatrix` is bits-only (no kernel-rule issue). The scale `s`
  drops out of the constant term (verified: F64 values unchanged to round-off).
- **Monotone degree**: Q ≥ Q' gives a degree ≥ that of Q', since the loop for Q passes through Q'. In 1-D all
  ghosts reach 3 at R = 3.5Δx for Q = 3–5, and Q at R ≥ 4.5Δx.

## Minor issues

- `boundary_conditions.md` "The fit", step 2 still describes "moves the donor ... for the requested degree". The
  kernel now lowers the degree step by step and tries a neighbour at each step. The commit message says "best-
  supported interior neighbour"; the code takes the one nearest to the target among those that carry the degree.
- `GeometricDomain` docstring: the signature line lacks `volume`, and "Points with `signed_distance(shape, x) <= 0`
  are interior" no longer holds exactly for balls (`_inside`).
- `_inside` is forwarded through `Tagged`/`Translated`/`Rotated` but not through union/intersection/setdiff, so a
  ball inside a combination still decides by `sqrt` (`misc_a.jl`: (0.6000000000000001, −0.8) is outside the ball,
  inside the union). Harmless; worth a comment.
- A closed shape on a periodic axis is still accepted (review A minor): its volume is computed as if closed.
- The builder's `Core.eval(Main, …)` falls back on any `UndefVarError`, including one raised inside a user
  constructor, and then re-evaluates in HyperCloudCore. Unusual, but it gives a confusing second error.
- Docs Stability item 0 and the commit message attribute B-F1 to the jitter alone (see N1).

## Verdict

The fixes for review A are correct, apart from the claim about very acute corners. Shape domains open along an
axis are now gridded, the volume is computed once, sphere grids are bitwise as before, cropping and tag 0 are
refused, the builder errors clearly, and `wall_geometry` is exact from 15° to 170°. For review B, the miscompile
workaround is confirmed to be what fixes the Float32 3-D case at 1/2/8 threads (the toolchain bug is still
present). The degree fallback is monotone, the 3-D cap removes the allocating path, and the KA compile time of 3-D
degree 3 dropped from 30 min to 10 s. The Float32 mirror fit is 10× more accurate, and KA parity and allocation
are unchanged. The point-generator change keeps the random-number order, leaves unjittered grids bitwise
unchanged, and moves only the points that crossed a wall. The full suite passes (25 556 tests).

However, B-F1 is fixed only for walls on the lattice. MirrorBC still fails to converge and grows in time whenever
a wall passes just outside a lattice row: curved walls, rotated obstacles and offset walls, jittered or not (N1).
The jitter of the wall row was one source of near-wall ghosts, not the cause.

New findings: 1 high (N1), 3 low (N2–N4); all confirmed (N3 by reading).
