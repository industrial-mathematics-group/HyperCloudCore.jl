Block States

The state of a particle is an SVector of M components, and M is a type parameter of every struct of the package: operations on a state unroll at compile time, which is what makes small systems fast and allocation free, and what makes large ones impossible. A discrete-velocity model with two species of $30^2$ velocities and the Chu reduction has 3600 components per particle: the compile time of the schemes grows faster than $M$, the per-slot flux buffers hold $(D + 1) M$ values per neighbour, and a kernel holding a 3600-wide SVector spills every register.

The block pipeline keeps the SVector pipeline as it is and composes it. It applies to block equations: systems whose transport is block diagonal, so that the components couple through source terms and per-particle reductions only. Every discrete-velocity model is one (each velocity is transported on its own, the collision couples them through the moments); a system whose flux Jacobian couples its components densely is not, and such systems are not the large ones in practice.

Block equations

A block equation is a HyperbolicPDE{D, W, T} whose state type is one block of width $W$, with $n_b$ blocks in total. It implements the three functions of the contract: num_blocks, block_flux and block_max_eigenvalue. Every HyperbolicPDE is a block equation with one block (the defaults forward to flux and max_eigenvalue), so the block pipeline reproduces the standard one for any equation, bit for bit.

BlockOf(eq, b) is block b as an ordinary PDE: flux and max_eigenvalue forward to the block functions with the block index as a run-time field, so a single compiled kernel serves every block. Constants a block's flux needs (the node velocities of a discrete-velocity block) can be given by block_data(eq, b): the stepper tabulates them once, and the flux pass gets BlockOf(eq, b, data) and calls block_flux(eq, U, b, data) with them read from the table. On a GPU, computing them in the kernel with a run-time index into tables inside the equation copied the whole equation to local memory in every thread.

The block state and the stepper

BlockState holds the $n_b$ blocks of all particles in one array of length $n_b N$ in block-major order (block $b$ of particle $i$ at index $(b - 1) N + i$): every block is a contiguous vector over the particles, the layout devices want. The width $W$ is a compile-time constant, the block count a run-time number.

BlockTimeStepper owns a GeneralRKTimeStepper for $W$ components and runs its stage kernels block by block on the views of the block state, with the equation wrapped as BlockOf(eq, b): the grid, the neighbour search, the MLS weights and the scheme's pre-pass are shared by all blocks. The stage functions of GeneralRKTimeStepper take their state arrays as arguments for that reason (the boundary conditions receive the reference state of the step start the same way, apply_boundary_conditions!); the stepper's own step advances any state array, pg.rhos by default.

eq  = MyDVM(...)                                   # a HyperbolicPDE{D, W, T} with num_blocks(eq) = nb
pg  = ParticleGrid(geom, dxs, weight, W)           # W components per particle
bs  = BlockState(eq, pg)
set_block_states!((x, b) -> initial_block(x, b), bs, pg)
ts  = BlockTimeStepper(eq, MUSCL(T, D, W, 2, MinmodLimiter(:hard), RusanovFlux()), RK3_SSP_Tableau(T))
dt  = 0.3 * get_time_step(pg, eq, ts.inner.divergence_interpolator, bs)
ts(eq, pg, bs, t, dt)
f_b = block_view(bs, b)                            # the states of block b

Source terms in two passes

A source of a block equation needs the whole distribution of a particle (the moments) but updates every block on its own. The contract of AbstractBlockSource splits it accordingly: block_reduce turns the blocks of a particle into a small bits value of parameters (for a BGK model the moments and the exponential parameters of the Maxwellian, for the mixture the species' velocities and temperatures after the stage), stored per particle in a BlockSourceTerm; block_source and block_implicit_solve then update one block from those parameters. The stepper runs the reduction once per stage and source (with dt_coeff = Δt a_ii for an implicit solve, 0 for an explicit evaluation) and the per-block pass after it. Implicit terms need an IMEX tableau; at its stages without an implicit diagonal they are evaluated explicitly, as in the standard stepper, and the right-hand side of a solved stage is $(U_{out} - U_{in}) / (\Delta t\, a_{ii})$. Several implicit terms are applied one after another (their increments add up).

MOOD on blocks

MOOD works as in the standard stepper, with the particle's order shared by all its blocks: at every checkpoint the flag pass runs once per block (the criterion on the block's components against the block's previous stage, the per-block admissibility that every block is finite), block_admissible adds the cross-block check (for a discrete-velocity model the positivity of the moments), and one drop pass lowers the order of every flagged particle; the previous stage's right-hand side is then recomputed on the affected particles for all blocks (the explicit sources reuse their parameters there) and the candidates are re-formed, until nothing is flagged. The Euler substeps of SSP tableaus, the forcing halos and the diagnostics (mood_flags, mood_inadmissible) are the same. MOODu2's curvature test reads the scheme's gradient cache, which holds the last block's gradients, so the block's gradients at the previous stage are gathered before its flag pass (one pre-pass per block and checkpoint).

Moving grids on blocks

On a moving grid the frame velocity of every particle is reduced before each flux pass: a prescribed mover gives it as usual, the Lagrangian movers through block_fluid_velocity (the mean velocity of the moments of a discrete-velocity model). The flux pass then runs in the frozen-velocity ALE frame of the particle, F − w_i U with the wave speed bound block_max_eigenvalue(eq, U, d, b, w_i) (the exact |λ − w| of a block's velocities makes the dissipation vanish where the block moves with the frame), the positions are stage variables as in the standard stepper and the volumes are re-estimated at the end of the step. The conservative Lagrangian frame of the standard stepper, which evaluates the frame velocity of every interface state, has no block form: a block cannot give the fluid velocity. Moving boundary pieces are not available yet.

Walls on blocks

The boundary conditions of the grid apply block by block with the block's state of the step start as the reference. A MirrorBC whose operator needs the whole distribution of a ghost (a diffuse kinetic wall: the density that returns the incident flux, summed over all velocities; a specular wall: a velocity reflected into another block) implements the wall in two passes, block_wall_reduce and block_wall_apply with the parameter type wall_reduction_type: the mirrored interior state of every block is fitted into a scratch array first (the even reflection), the reduction runs once per ghost over the mirrored blocks, and the application gives every block's ghost state from its mirrored value, the parameters and, where it needs them, the other mirrored blocks of the ghost. An operator without a reduction acts block by block.

Adaptivity, reordering and moving pieces on blocks

The host routines that change the particle set take further state arrays and treat them exactly like the grid's own state (the geometric decisions are shared, the remap of every array is that of the single state, the compaction and the new particles' states follow): adapt_particles!(pg, ad, order; states), sweep_boundaries!(pg, t_old, t, order; states) and the reordering pg.reorder(pg; extra). The block pipeline uses them with its blocks: adapt_particles!(pg, ad, order, bs) between steps (the blocks are copied into per-block vectors around an event, since the block-major layout depends on the particle count, and laid out again afterwards; nothing is copied when no pair violates the spacing rules), the moving pieces inside the step (the ghosts placed at every stage, the sweep at the end with every block remapped), and reorder!(pg, bs). Checked: with a compressing prescribed mover and the adaptivity, with a piston sweeping a tube, the block runs reproduce the standard runs bit for bit, particle set included.

One launch per pass

Every pass of the stepper runs in one launch over the (particle, block) pairs (foreach_block: nb · N work-items, the stage kernels of the standard stepper on the views of the blocks, consecutive work-items being consecutive particles of one block as the block-major layout stores them): the predictor, the implicit and explicit sources, the Euler substep, the MOOD flag pass, the wall application and the package's boundary conditions (FixedDirichlet; OutflowBC with its donor search once per tag, every block copying from the root of the donor chain; ExtrapolationBC and MirrorBC with the geometry of the fit, the donor, the degree and the Cholesky factor of the moment matrix, computed once per ghost into a scratch of the stepper and the fit per (ghost, block) from it), the CFL step of the state (get_time_step of a block state, into its cfl buffer), and the plateau ranges of MOODu2 in one reduction per launch. The passes that use per-particle scratch of the scheme, the pre-pass and the flux pass (the interaction buffer, the gradient cache) and the MOODu2 pre-pass, run over blocks_per_launch blocks at a time (the keyword of the stepper; :auto, the default, is 256 ÷ W blocks, at least one, so that a launch covers about 256 components whatever the width; 0: all blocks) with the scratch replicated once per block of the launch, which memory_estimate prices (at W = 8 the default 32 costs 32 times the scheme's slot buffers and gradient cache of one block; a width of 60 with 32 blocks per launch ran out of memory on a 16 GB GPU). A launch of N work-items per block, as the first version ran, never filled a GPU and paid the launch latency once per block and pass: the 2-D orifice of the mixture study (5.9k particles, 450 blocks of 8) launched 27 600 kernels per step. Any other boundary condition still runs block by block through its functor.

Devices and the velocity decomposition

The block pipeline runs on any storage: the block state, the stepper's buffers, the parameter arrays of the sources and of the walls are Growables of the storage array type (the stepper moves the parameter arrays of its sources to the storage of its stage buffers), the kernels are those of the standard stepper, and the host-side passes (the initial conditions, the snapshots, the adaptivity and the sweep of moving pieces) copy blocks between the device and the host, the last two only when an event changes the particle set. memory_estimate and reserve! take the block stepper and the block state (reserve!(pg, ts, bs, scheme; limit)): the state arrays count nb elements per particle, and with the kinetic walls of the grid the block-major scratch of the mirrored states and the wall parameters count too. Checked on the KernelAbstractions CPU backend (PoCL) and with CUDA.

A state split over devices

BlockDevices runs a block state whose blocks are split over several devices (the velocity decomposition: one node with several GPUs, each holding the whole cloud and a range of blocks, driven from one process). Every part (BlockPart) is a grid on its device, a BlockTimeStepper and a BlockState built for its block range (the blocks keyword of both), and a callable that makes the device current. The flux of a block depends on that block alone, so the right-hand side needs no exchange; what couples the blocks are per-particle reductions, and the step runs the phases of the single stepper in lockstep over the parts, exchanging their partial sums through host buffers (summed in block order, (D + 2) N numbers at a time):

  • the sources, in the additive form of reduction_partial_type, block_partial (the contribution of a block range, which adds up over ranges: the moments of those velocities) and finalize_reduction (the parameters from the sum); a source that gives it gets block_reduce for free, so the single stepper runs the same arithmetic;
  • the frame velocity of a Lagrangian mover (fluid_velocity_partial_type, block_fluid_velocity_partial, finalize_fluid_velocity: density and momentum) and the cross-block admissibility of MOOD (admissible_partial_type, block_admissible_partial, finalize_admissible), each with the same fallback to the direct form;
  • the MOOD flags, OR-ed over the parts before the drop pass that every part runs (the same orders everywhere);
  • for a kinetic wall, the mirrored ghost rows of all blocks, gathered per tag (nb × ghosts states, the ghosts being few) and read through the same indices as the whole scratch: every part then runs the operator's reduction and application on them unchanged, so the walls are those of the single stepper bit for bit (no additive form of a wall is needed; a specular reflection's flux balance reads the whole distribution twice);
  • host_reduction gives a user's per-particle partial (a snapshot's moments) summed over the parts.

An equation that implements block_admissible without the additive form is refused, as are moving boundary pieces, the particle adaptivity and the reordering (host events of the single stepper). The result agrees with the single stepper to round-off (the partials are added in a different order). Checked with host parts: two and three parts of a four-block transport with the implicit mean relaxation, with inflow and outflow, with MOOD u1 and the cross-block admissibility (the same flags and orders on every part), with a Lagrangian mover and with reflecting walls whose operator reads the mirror partner's block from another part, all within 1e-12 of the single stepper. A device run needs several GPUs; the exchange goes through the host.

Scope of this version: host and single-device storage, and a split state over devices of one process. Checked against the standard pipeline: the number of launches of a step does not depend on the number of blocks, every chunking of the passes with scratch gives the standard run bit for bit, a one-block Burgers run is bit-identical, a discrete transport with four blocks of two velocities reproduces the eight-component run to round-off (bit for bit with MinmodLimiter; the Venkatakrishnan limiter's tolerance depends on the state vector as a whole) and converges to the exact solution at second order, in 1-D and 2-D, with inflow and outflow conditions, on the Polyester and the KernelAbstractions CPU backends. With a source that needs the reduction (every component relaxing towards the particle's mean, the structure of the BGK collision) the block run agrees with the standard stepper and the equivalent AbstractImplicitSourceTerm to round-off (the stage states and implicit right-hand sides bit for bit, the flux pass to an ulp), with ARS222 (also in the stiff limit) and with an explicit tableau and source. With MOOD (MOODu1 on a box per component with the unlimited third-order scheme, the Euler substeps of RK3, the forcing halo, ARS222 with a source, OnlyMOOD, and the cross-block admissibility of a guarded equation) the same particles drop their orders as in the standard run and the states agree to round-off.

Checked: a prescribed mover gives the standard run bit for bit (positions and volumes included); a Lagrangian mover with one common velocity transports every state exactly; a reflecting wall operator that reads the mirror partner's block reproduces the permutation operator of the standard run to round-off.

What comes next: the exchange of a split state through device-to-device copies instead of the host, and a conservative Lagrangian frame on blocks, which needs the frame velocity of a pair from the particles' velocities (a design question: w_ij = ½ (w_i + w_j) from the reduction instead of the interface states' velocities).

The contract of a block equation: a system of nb · W components whose transport is block diagonal, so that block b (components (b − 1) W + 1 … b W, a State{W, T}) has a flux and wave speeds of its own that depend on that block only. The components couple through source terms and through per-particle reductions only (discrete-velocity models: every velocity is transported on its own, the collision couples them through the moments). Such an equation is a HyperbolicPDE{D, W, T} (its state type is the block) that implements num_blocks, block_flux and block_max_eigenvalue; the BlockTimeStepper runs the schemes of the package on every block with the same grid, stencils and weights.

Every HyperbolicPDE is a block equation with one block: the defaults are num_blocks = 1, block_flux = flux and block_max_eigenvalue = max_eigenvalue, so the block pipeline reproduces the standard one for any equation.

HyperCloudCore.BlockTimeStepper — Type
BlockTimeStepper(eq, div_interp, mood, sources::Tuple, tableau; storage = Array, kwargs...)
BlockTimeStepper(eq, div_interp, sources::Tuple, tableau; kwargs...)
BlockTimeStepper(eq, div_interp, tableau; kwargs...)
(ts::BlockTimeStepper)(eq, pg, bs::BlockState, time, dt)

The time integrator of the block pipeline: the stage kernels of GeneralRKTimeStepper run block by block on the views of a BlockState, with the equation wrapped as BlockOf(eq, b), so the grid, the neighbour search, the MLS weights, the scheme and its pre-pass are shared by all blocks and the state length nb · W is a run-time number. The stepper owns a GeneralRKTimeStepper for W components (inner: the interpolator, MOOD scratch, buffers and options; its stage buffers are sized to nb · N), and the kwargs are its options (mood_substeps, reset_orders_per_stage, storage, …).

Every pass runs in one launch over all blocks (foreach_block: nb · N work-items, the stage kernels of the standard stepper on the views of the blocks), except the passes that use per-particle scratch of the scheme (the pre-pass and the flux pass with the interaction buffer and the gradient cache, the MOODu2 pre-pass), which run over blocks_per_launch blocks at a time with the scratch replicated that many times (:auto, the default: 256 ÷ W blocks, at least one, so that a launch covers about 256 components whatever the width and the scratch stays about 256/W times the scheme's slot and gradient buffers of one block, see memory_estimate; 0: all blocks at once; a width of 60 with 32 blocks per launch ran out of memory on a 16 GB GPU where 8 with 32 did not). A launch of N work-items per block never filled a GPU and paid the launch latency nb times per pass.

blocks is the range of blocks the stepper runs (all of the equation by default; a range for the part of a state split over devices, see BlockDevices: such a stepper steps through the devices object only).

sources are BlockSourceTerms. Explicit ones enter the right-hand side of every stage after a reduction over the blocks at the stage state; implicit ones are solved at the stages with an implicit diagonal (a_ii ≠ 0: the reduction with dt_coeff = Δt a_ii, then the solve per block, the right-hand side K_I = (U_out − U_in) / (Δt a_ii); several implicit terms one after another, as in the standard stepper) and evaluated explicitly at the others; they need an IMEX tableau. The explicit part of the tableau may be any explicit RK method.

mood works as in the standard stepper (the stage-wise fixed point, the Euler substeps of SSP tableaus, the forcing halos), with the particle's order shared by all its blocks: the criterion (MOODu1, MOODu2, OnlyMOOD, MOODPAD) checks every block's components, the per-block admissibility is block_is_admissible, and block_admissible adds the cross-block check (e.g. positive moments); a flagged particle drops one order, and the previous stage's right-hand side is recomputed on the affected particles for all blocks. MOODu2's curvature test reads the scheme's gradient cache, so the block's gradients at the previous stage are gathered before its flag pass (one pre-pass per block and checkpoint).

On a moving grid (pg.mover) the frame velocity of every particle is reduced before each flux pass (block_fluid_velocity for the Lagrangian movers, the mover's velocity otherwise) and the pass runs in the frozen-velocity ALE frame of the particle; the positions are stage variables as in the standard stepper, the volumes are re-estimated at the end of the step. (The conservative Lagrangian frame of the standard stepper needs the frame velocity of every interface state, which a block cannot give.) Moving boundary pieces are placed and swept as in the standard stepper, the sweep remapping every block (sweep_boundaries! with the blocks as state arrays); adapt_particles!(pg, ad, order, bs) is the particle adaptivity between steps and reorder!(pg, bs) the spatial reordering. The boundary conditions of the grid apply per block with the block's state of the step start as the reference: the package's conditions (FixedDirichlet, OutflowBC, ExtrapolationBC, MirrorBC) in one launch over the blocks (the outflow's donor search once per tag; the ghost fits with the geometry of the fit, the donor, the degree and the Cholesky factor, once per ghost and the fit per (ghost, block)), any other condition block by block through its functor; the CFL step of the state likewise; a MirrorBC whose operator reduces over the blocks (block_wall_reduce) runs in the pipeline's passes. For one block (num_blocks(eq) == 1) the result is bit-identical to GeneralRKTimeStepper without sources, and agrees to round-off with the equivalent sources (a separately compiled flux pass).

AbstractBlockSource
AbstractExplicitBlockSource <: AbstractBlockSource
AbstractImplicitBlockSource <: AbstractBlockSource

A source term of a block equation acts in two passes: a reduction over all blocks of a particle into a small bits value of per-particle parameters (for a discrete-velocity model the moments and what follows from them, e.g. the exponential parameters of the Maxwellians and the stiffness), then the update of every block from those parameters. The contract, all kernel functions:

  • reduction_type(st) -> Type{P}: the (bits) type of the parameters.
  • block_reduce(st, p, Y, N, nb, pg, t, dt_coeff) -> P: the parameters of particle p from the full state array Y (block b of particle p at Y[(b - 1) N + p], nb blocks). For an implicit term dt_coeff is Δt a_ii of the stage (the parameters of the solved stage, e.g. the Maxwellian of the moments after the collision); it is 0 when the term is evaluated explicitly (the parameters of the current state).
  • block_source(st, U::State{W}, b, params::P, p, pg, t) -> State{W}: the source of block b (explicit terms; implicit terms implement it too, for the stages of an IMEX tableau without an implicit diagonal).
  • block_implicit_solve(st, U::State{W}, b, params::P, dt_coeff, p, pg, t) -> State{W}: the solution of the implicit stage equation U_out = U + dt_coeff S(U_out) for block b (implicit terms).

A term is wrapped in a BlockSourceTerm, which owns the parameter array, and handed to the BlockTimeStepper. Boundary particles get no source and no parameters.

HyperCloudCore.reduction_partial_type — Function
reduction_partial_type(st) -> Type or nothing
block_partial(st, p, Y, N, nb, blocks::UnitRange, pg, t) -> partial
finalize_reduction(st, p, partial, pg, t, dt_coeff) -> params

The additive form of a block source's reduction, for a state whose blocks are split over several devices: the contribution of the blocks blocks of particle p is a bits value that adds up over block ranges (+; for a discrete-velocity model the moments of those velocities), and finalize_reduction turns the sum over all blocks into the parameters of block_reduce (the Maxwellian, the stiffness, …). A source that gives reduction_partial_type (default nothing: the reduction reads all blocks at once) gets block_reduce for free, finalize_reduction(st, p, block_partial(st, p, Y, N, nb, 1:nb, pg, t), pg, t, dt_coeff) (the default block_reduce). The decomposition of a block state over devices exchanges these partials only (one per particle and device); the stepper of one device still runs the whole reduction.

HyperCloudCore.BlockDevices — Type
BlockDevices(eq, parts::Vector{<:BlockPart})
(devs::BlockDevices)(time, dt)
get_time_step(devs::BlockDevices; cached_weights = true)

A block state of the block equation eq split over devices: parts hold consecutive block ranges that cover 1:num_blocks(eq) in order, each on its device (its storage) with the whole cloud, its stepper and its state. A step runs the phases of BlockTimeStepper in lockstep over the parts and exchanges, through host buffers, the per-particle partial sums of the reductions, the MOOD flags and the mirrored ghost rows of the kinetic walls:

  • the sources give their reduction in the additive form (reduction_partial_type), the frame velocity of a Lagrangian mover in fluid_velocity_partial_type, the cross-block admissibility of MOOD in admissible_partial_type (an equation that implements block_admissible without it is refused); the sums are formed in block order on the host;
  • the MOOD flag pass runs per part, the flags are OR-ed over the parts, the drop pass runs on every part (the same orders everywhere), as does every host-side decision;
  • a kinetic wall: the mirrored ghost rows of all blocks are gathered per tag (nb × ghosts states, exchanged per application), and every part runs the operator's reduction (block_wall_reduce, unchanged) and its application on them, so the walls are those of the single stepper bit for bit.

The parts' device work runs concurrently, one task per part, when every part has its own device (an activate) and Julia runs with several threads (start it with at least as many threads as parts); the exchanges in between run on the host. The parts' grids are copies of one cloud (a device_grid per device; copies, not the shared arrays of device_grid(Array, pg): every part moves its own positions and orders). Not supported on a split state: moving boundary pieces, the particle adaptivity and the reordering (host events of the single stepper). The result agrees with the single stepper to round-off (the partial sums are added in a different order).
