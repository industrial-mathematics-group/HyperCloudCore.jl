Particle Volumes and Adaptivity

Collocation particles carry no mass. To define a discrete conserved quantity, and to make inserting and merging particles exactly conservative, every interior particle gets a positive weight $V_i$ (stored in pg.core.volumes; the ghost particles outside $\Omega$ get none) with

\[\sum_i V_i = |\Omega|, \qquad \sum_i V_i\, f(x_i) \approx \int_\Omega f\,\mathrm dx .\]

No Voronoi tessellation is built or stored. $V_i = \int_\Omega \phi_i\,\mathrm dx$ for shape functions $\phi_i$ that form a partition of unity ($\sum_i \phi_i \equiv 1$), evaluated with a quadrature on the point cloud itself or on a throwaway lattice. Only the scalar $V_i$ is kept.

The orders are those of $\sum_i V_i f(x_i) \to \int_\Omega f$ over the interior particles, measured with $R = 2.5\Delta x$ and a smooth, non-harmonic $f$ (1-D: $N = 40 \dots 320$; 2-D: $N = 20 \dots 160$; for a harmonic $f$ such as $e^x \cos y$ the leading term of the midpoint rule, proportional to $\int \Delta f$, vanishes and the errors look one to two orders smaller). On a periodic cloud there are no ghosts. On a bounded region only the interior particles carry volume: the final renormalisation makes them sum to $\lvert\Omega\rvert$ exactly, the ghosts get $V = 0$, and MLS forms its partition of unity from the interior particles only, so its reproduction holds up to the wall; what is left is the lattice's midpoint rule, second order for every $P$. Shepard's particle-based density gives a particle on the wall a whole cell, hence its first order there (relative errors of about $0.1$–$1\,h$, depending on $f$ and the domain: $1.1\,h$ for $x^2$ on $[-1, 1]$).

Shepard on a jittered periodic cloud: error about $2\cdot 10^{-3}$ at 30 % jitter for every $N$; MLS$(P \ge 1)$ converges there at order 2–4.

Shepard starts from the kernel-density volume $V_i \propto 1/S_i$, $S_i = w(0) + \sum_j w_{ij}$, applies sweeps partition-of-unity sweeps $V_i \leftarrow \sum_j (w_{ij}/S_j)\,V_j$ (which preserve $\sum V$), zeroes the ghosts and renormalises the interior to $|\Omega|$. On a uniform periodic cloud it reproduces $V_i = \Delta x^D$ exactly. It needs a kernel that is bounded at zero distance; with InverseWeightFunction the volumes fall back to $|\Omega|/N$ with a warning.

\[S_i\]

is a lattice sum over a fixed number of neighbours, which is not proportional to the local particle density when the spacing varies. On a moving (stretched) cloud the Shepard quadrature therefore has a bias that depends on the local stretch and does not shrink with the resolution: for a smooth profile on a 1-D grid moved by a sinusoidal velocity of amplitude 0.5, $\sum_i V_i u(x_i)$ of the exact solution is off by $-2\cdot 10^{-3}$ at every $N$ from 200 to 1600, while MLS volumes of degree 2 converge ($-2\cdot 10^{-5} \to 5\cdot 10^{-9}$). Mass errors measured with Shepard volumes on a moving grid contain that bias.

MLS evaluates, at every lattice point $y$, the shape functions $\phi_i(y) = w_i\, p_i^{\mathsf T} a$ with $\big(\sum_k w_k p_k p_k^{\mathsf T}\big) a = e_1$ and the full basis $p_k = (1, \text{monomials}(x_k - y))$. Because $\sum_i \phi_i(y)\, q(x_i) = q(y)$ for polynomials $q$ of degree $\le P$, the volumes integrate such polynomials exactly (up to the lattice's midpoint error). P = 0 is the lattice-integrated Shepard partition. Shape functions of degree $\ge 1$ can be negative; a volume that comes out negative (rare without ghosts in the partition) is clamped before the final renormalisation. The lattice sweep is a serial host loop: as the per-step re-estimation of a moving grid it costs about 3 (1-D, 1600 particles) to 21 (2-D, $141^2$) explicit MUSCL2/RK2 steps on 8 threads, so it is not the default. On a device grid it runs on a host copy (only the volumes are copied back).

pg = ParticleGrid(geom, dx, wf, M; volume_estimator = MLSVolumes(2))   # at construction
update_volumes!(pg, ShepardVolumes())                                   # any later time
total_mass(pg)                                                          # Σ V_i U_i
domain_volume(pg)                                                       # |Ω|

domain_volume is analytic for the built-in rectangular and spherical domains; define a method for a custom geometry label.

Volumes on moving grids

After every step of a moving grid, the time stepper recomputes the volumes from the new geometry with its volume_estimator (default ShepardVolumes(); nothing leaves them untouched). With the config-driven builders the estimator of the Grid namespace (:volumes) is used for the initial volumes and for every re-estimation (build_particle_grid stores it in context[:VolumeEstimator], which build_timestepper and build_adaptivity read). Recomputation keeps the volumes consistent with the current cloud at the cost of one neighbour sweep, and never drifts. The content form of the Lagrangian movers with MUSCL (moving grids) does not re-estimate: its volumes are integrated from the scheme's own volume rate $\dot V_i = V_i\,(\nabla\cdot w)_i$ (the geometric conservation law, smooth in time, with $\sum V_i U_i$ exactly conserved), and $\sum_i V_i$ drifts from $\lvert\Omega\rvert$ by the discretisation error of the divergence; the events of the particle adaptivity re-estimate the volumes they touch.

The collocation scheme itself is not exactly conservative, with or without motion: $\sum_i V_i U_i$ changes by the scheme's truncation error per step. What the volumes buy is a well-defined discrete mass, so that topology changes can preserve it exactly.

Particle insertion and merging

On Lagrangian grids particles cluster in compressions and thin out in expansions. ParticleAdaptivity repairs that between steps:

ad = ParticleAdaptivity(Float64; max_gap = 1.6, min_gap = 0.5, clearance = 0.7,   # (the defaults in 1-D and 2-D)
                        samples_per_dx = 2, exclude_reduced_order = true,
                        max_events = typemax(Int), max_rounds = 8,
                        volume_estimator = ts.volume_estimator)   # moving grid: the stepper's estimator
report = adapt_particles!(pg, ad, _extract_order(interp))   # between time steps
report.inserted, report.removed, report.mass_residual         # residual at round-off
report.escaped, report.held, report.seeded, report.refused    # open boundaries, events left out (below)

Detection

Lengths are in units of $\Delta x = \min_d \Delta x_d$.

  • Insertion: a neighbour pair farther apart than max_gap whose midpoint has no particle within clearance gets a new particle at the midpoint. The clearance test is what prevents insertions on a regular lattice, where second neighbours are $2\Delta x$ apart but the midpoint is occupied. The defaults depend on the dimension: max_gap 1.6 and clearance 0.7 in 1-D and 2-D, $1.6\sqrt{D/2}$ and $0.7\sqrt{D/2}$ above (1.96 and 0.86 in 3-D), because the longest diagonal of a lattice cell, $\sqrt D\,\Delta x$, must stay below max_gap or its midpoint (the cell centre, $\tfrac{\sqrt D}{2}\Delta x$ from the corners) within the clearance: with the 2-D values a 3-D lattice got a particle at every cell centre.
  • Merge: a neighbour pair closer than min_gap is replaced by one particle at its midpoint.
  • At most one event per particle neighbourhood and per round, and at most max_events per round. Event centres are kept $(R + \Delta x/2)\sqrt D + \tfrac32 R + \tfrac32 \Delta x$ apart, so that no event's quadrature sees a particle that another event of the round adds or removes: the events of a round are remapped one after the other on the old topology. Their local sets (the particles a remap writes) may overlap: every remap is exactly conservative on its own set, runs on the values the earlier ones wrote, and knows the particles the others remove, which are no part of its balance. (Up to 0.9.9 the whole local sets were kept apart, $R\,(2 + 2\sqrt D) + \Delta x$: 13 instead of 9.5 spacings in 2-D at $R = 2.5\,\Delta x$, i.e. half the events per round. With the still older spacing of $4R$ and no knowledge of the other events' removals, calls with several events lost up to $10^{-7}$ of the mass.)
  • Rounds: a call repeats detection, remaps, topology change and neighbour search until no pair violates the rules, at most max_rounds times (default 8; the volumes are re-estimated once, at the end). Between the rounds the neighbour lists are repaired, not searched again: a particle that neither lost nor gained a neighbour keeps its list (renumbered), and the lists of the particles next to the events are written again from the rebuilt cell list, entry for entry those of a full search; one full search at the end of the call restores the compact slot layout. A round is therefore cheap, and a smaller limit saves little while it can starve an expanding region: on a 3-D blast at 64³, 3 instead of 8 rounds took a call from 0.32 to 0.26 time steps and the fewest neighbours of a particle from 37 to 7. The spacing above allows a few events per $(9.5\,\Delta x)^2$ and round in 2-D at $R = 2.5\,\Delta x$, and a pair is only found while it is a neighbour pair: a gap between max_gap and $R$ that is not served in time grows beyond $R$ and is never filled. With one round per step (up to 0.9.9) the centre of a Sedov blast at $R = 2.5\,\Delta x$ lost its stencils (2 neighbours) and the run blew up at $t = 0.1$; with rounds it keeps 14 and completes.
  • Not outside the region: no event is centred outside the region (the midpoint of a pair can lie there: it would be an interior particle without a volume, with the state 0/0), and an event that cannot be remapped is left out (report.refused): one next to a particle without a usable volume, or whose new particle gets no share of the region. Such events used to stop the run with "Remap produced no volume".
  • Shock band: with exclude_reduced_order, no event happens within $4R$ of a particle whose MOOD order was lowered during the last step. Interpolation of any order is unreliable across a discontinuity, and a Lagrangian grid in a shock region is better left alone (or driven towards Eulerian motion by the mover).

Open boundaries

On a moving cloud (a grid mover) the call first keeps the cloud inside the region and up to its boundary:

  • Escaped particles. An interior particle that has left the region is removed with its content, the outflow (report.escaped, report.escaped_content). Up to 0.9.9 such particles stayed outside (37 % of the cloud of the 2-D Riemann problem C6 by $t = 0.3$), kept interacting with the ghosts and with each other, and got no volume from an estimator. A particle that crossed a wall is put back onto the wall instead (report.held): a MirrorBC of a shape-based domain, or any condition type that declares is_wall. Only particles with a ghost among their neighbours (or with none) are tested, so the test costs one kernel on a cloud that stays inside.
  • Seeds. Where an interior particle and a ghost among its neighbours are farther apart than max_gap, with the midpoint inside the region and free within clearance, a new interior particle is put at the midpoint (report.seeded): the cloud receded from the boundary, an inflow. Its state is the mean of the pair's (the ghost's is the boundary datum), its volume the estimator's (the nominal $\prod_d \Delta x_d$ without one). A seed is not a remap event: the gap is volume that no particle holds, and its content enters the region (report.seeded_content). A whole row is seeded in one call.

What leaves and what the seeds bring is not part of mass_residual: the interior content changes by seeded_content - escaped_content + mass_residual over the call, with the residual at round-off.

The conservative remap

A topology change replaces the partition of unity $\{\phi_i^{\text{old}}\}$ by $\{\phi_i^{\text{new}}\}$. Each particle's volume and content change by the swept amounts

\[\delta V_i = \int \big(\phi_i^{\text{new}} - \phi_i^{\text{old}}\big)\,\mathrm dx, \qquad \delta m_i = \int \big(\phi_i^{\text{new}} - \phi_i^{\text{old}}\big)\,\tilde U\,\mathrm dx ,\]

where $\tilde U$ is the limited (bound-clipped) linear reconstruction of the old solution around the nearest old particle. New particles have $\phi^{\text{old}} = 0$, removed particles $\phi^{\text{new}} = 0$. Since both families sum to one pointwise, $\sum_i \delta V_i = 0$ and $\sum_i \delta m_i = 0$ for any quadrature: conservation is exact by construction, which is the particle analogue of swept-region remapping in ALE finite volumes. The integrals are evaluated on a lattice of spacing $\Delta x/\texttt{samples\_per\_dx}$ over the box of half-width $2R$ around the event, using only the old neighbour structure. The new state is $U_i = (V_i U_i + \delta m_i)/(V_i + \delta V_i)$.

Two corrections follow:

A survivor's volume changes by its swept share relative to the larger of its volume and its share of the quadrature box: $V_i \leftarrow V_i + \delta V_i \min(1, V_i / \int_{\text{box}} \phi_i^{\text{old}})$, and its swept content is scaled alike. For volumes that are the partition's this is $V_i + \delta V_i$. Volumes that are not (an estimator of higher degree, the scheme's own of the content form) can be smaller than the share an event takes away; the former $\max(V_i + \delta V_i,\ 10^{-12} V_i)$ then left volumes of $10^{-12} V_i$ with a negative content, and repeated events volumes of $10^{-148}$ (Sedov). The residual step keeps both sums exact.

  1. Residual. The stored content of a removed particle, $V_r U_r$, is not exactly $\int \phi_r^{\text{old}} \tilde U$. The difference is spread over the affected particles in proportion to their new volumes, so $\sum V U$ is preserved to round-off.
  2. Repair. Each affected particle is clipped to the bounds of its old neighbourhood, and the clipped content is redistributed to affected particles that still have room. This enforces the discrete maximum principle while keeping the sum exact; if no room is left, the remainder is spread by volume and the bounds are violated by that remainder.

Boundary particles (the ghosts of a non-periodic box) are not remapped: their states are boundary data (FixedDirichlet restores them from pg.rhos, OutflowBC overwrites them at the next stage), so an event next to a wall leaves their states and volumes as they are. Their shape functions still change near the event; that swept share is left out of the balance, so the residual step puts it onto the interior particles and $\sum V U$ over the interior (the whole content, since ghosts carry no volume) is preserved exactly; mass_residual reports the change of the interior content. (Up to version 0.7.9 the ghosts were remapped like interior particles, which changed Dirichlet data permanently.) Events only start at interior particles, and insertions need two interior particles.

Then the particle arrays are compacted/extended, the neighbour lists are rebuilt, and new particles start at the scheme's maximum order. The remap is second-order accurate in smooth regions (linear reconstruction, averaged over the new shape function's support).

Volumes after the call

The remap's volumes $V_i + \delta V_i$ are integrals of the Shepard partition. A moving grid re-estimates the volumes after the next step with its own estimator, which defines volumes differently (per particle, the two differ by several percent near an event), and that re-estimation would change $\sum V U$ by about as much as an event moves. With volume_estimator set (the builders pass the stepper's estimator on moving grids), adapt_particles! therefore re-estimates the volumes right after the topology change and puts the resulting change of the interior $\sum V U$ back onto the particles the remap changed (not with own_volumes = true, for a stepper that keeps its volumes between the steps, keeps_own_volumes: there the remap's volumes stay, $\sum V U$ is exact without a correction, and only the seeds are given the estimator's volume; replacing all volumes, as up to 0.9.9, put the mismatch between the scheme's and the estimator's volumes of the whole cloud onto the changed particles), with the bounded, exactly conservative redistribution of the repair above. The call is then exact for the volumes the following steps use, and the states differ from the remap's only by that small correction. Ghost particles carry no volume, so the interior content is the whole content and total_mass stays exact as well. (Up to version 0.7.9 the ghosts had volumes and their content change was put onto the interior particles, i.e. moved across the wall: about $10^{-3}$ of the mass per event next to a wall; in 0.8 it stayed with the ghosts.) (Keeping each particle's content $V_i U_i$ across the re-estimation would also be exact, but it puts the per-particle volume differences, up to 18 % next to a merge, into the states.)

Cost

A call on a well-spaced cloud inside its region costs two kernels (the interior particles next to the boundary, the pairs that violate the rules). Otherwise the serial passes visit the flagged particles only, the candidates of later rounds follow from those of the first (the particles left, the neighbours of what was removed, the new particles and their neighbours), the remap's scratch is one neighbour list, and with own_volumes no estimator runs over the cloud. The clearance tests use the stored distance vectors of the neighbour lists, and the lists are repaired between the rounds. What the remap does not do

  • It does not change states outside the event's $3R$–$4R$ neighbourhood, so far-field solutions are untouched (with volume_estimator, far-field volumes change by the estimator's global renormalisation, typically $O(1/N)$; not with own_volumes).
  • It does not conserve across the time step itself; see above.
  • It does not move particles for mesh quality. A repulsion or smoothing term belongs in the GridMover, and keeps the particle set well-spaced before adaptivity has to act.

Reference

HyperCloudCore.MLSVolumes — Type
MLSVolumes(P; samples_per_dx = 2)

V_i = ∫_Ω φ_i dx with MLS shape functions reproducing polynomials of degree P, integrated by a midpoint rule on a background lattice with samples_per_dx points per particle spacing (the lattice is not stored). P = 0 is the background-integrated Shepard partition.

The shape functions reproduce polynomials of degree P, and on periodic clouds the quadrature Σ V_i f(x_i) converges at about order P + 1 for P ≥ 1, also on jittered and moving clouds (the discrete mass of a moving grid converges; P = 0 is first order on jittered clouds). On a bounded region the partition is formed by the interior particles only (ghosts get no volume), so the reproduction holds up to the wall, and the interior volumes sum to |Ω| exactly. The quadrature is then limited by the lattice's midpoint rule: order 2 in 1-D and 2-D for every P (unit square, exp(x + y), N = 20 → 80: P = 0 6.3e-4 → 3.8e-5, P = 1 3.0e-3 → 2.0e-4, P = 2 5.1e-5 → 3.3e-6; P = 1 is the least accurate on a box). The lattice sweep is a serial host loop: as the re-estimation of a moving grid it costs about 3 (1-D) to 20 (2-D) explicit time steps of a low-order scheme. On a device grid it runs on a host copy (only the volumes are copied back).

source
HyperCloudCore.ShepardVolumes — Type
ShepardVolumes(; sweeps = 1)

Kernel-density volumes V_i ∝ 1 / S_i, S_i = w(0) + Σ_j w_ij, followed by sweeps partition-of-unity smoothing sweeps V_i ← Σ_j (w_ij / S_j) V_j (which preserve Σ V exactly), and a final renormalisation so that the interior volumes sum to |Ω| (ghost particles get no volume). Requires a kernel that is finite at zero distance (ExponentialWeightFunction; InverseWeightFunction is not suitable).

Exact (V_i = Δx^D) on a uniform periodic lattice. On a bounded box the quadrature Σ V_i f(x_i) over the interior is first order (a particle on the wall gets a whole cell: relative errors of about 0.1-0.5 h, e.g. 1.4e-2 for x² on [0, 1] and 4.4e-3 for exp(x + y) on the unit square at N = 40). On a jittered periodic cloud it does not converge reliably (error about 2e-3 for N = 40…320 at 30 % jitter). On a cloud whose spacing varies smoothly (a moving grid) S_i is a lattice sum over a fixed number of neighbours, which is not proportional to the local density, so Σ V_i f(x_i) has a bias that depends on the local stretch and does not shrink with the resolution (−2e-3 for a smooth profile on a grid stretched by ±50 %, at every N); use MLSVolumes where the discrete mass of a moving grid matters, at a higher cost per update.

(Up to 0.8 the ghosts got volumes too and the renormalisation ran over all particles, so the interior volumes summed to less than |Ω| by the ghosts' share: 4 % in 1-D at h = 1/160, 30-40 % on a 2-D box at N = 20.)

domain_volume(geom::GeometricDomain) -> T

Volume of the physical region. Analytic for boxes and balls, shape_volume of the shape of every other shape-based domain (a lattice integration unless the shape defines its volume); define a method for custom geometry labels without a shape (or integrate geom.is_interior on a lattice).

HyperCloudCore.interior_mass — Method
interior_mass(pg, U = pg.rhos) -> State{M, T}

Σ V_i U_i over the interior particles (boundary/ghost particles excluded): the content of the domain, which the adaptivity keeps exact. Equal to total_mass as long as the ghost volumes are zero (every estimator sets them so). A reduction like total_mass, so it works on device grids as well.

HyperCloudCore.total_mass — Method
total_mass(pg, U = pg.rhos) -> State{M, T}

Discrete conserved content Σ_i V_i U_i over all particles of the current cloud (U a state array over the cloud). Ghost particles carry no volume, so this is the content of the region (see also interior_mass, which skips them explicitly).

HyperCloudCore.ParticleAdaptivity — Type
ParticleAdaptivity(; max_gap = nothing, min_gap = 0.5, clearance = nothing, samples_per_dx = 2,
                     exclude_reduced_order = true, max_events = typemax(Int), max_rounds = 8,
                     volume_estimator = nothing)

Rules for keeping a moving point cloud well-spaced. Lengths are multiples of the particle spacing dx = minimum(pg.meta.dx).

  • Insertion: a neighbour pair (i, j) farther apart than max_gap·dx whose midpoint has no particle within clearance·dx gets a new particle at the midpoint.
  • Merge: a neighbour pair closer than min_gap·dx is replaced by one particle at the midpoint.
  • max_gap = nothing, clearance = nothing: by the dimension of the grid, 1.6 and 0.7 in 1-D and 2-D, 1.6 √(D/2) and 0.7 √(D/2) above (1.96 and 0.86 in 3-D). A regular lattice must not trigger insertions: its longest diagonal, √D dx, has to stay below max_gap, or its midpoint, the cell centre at √D/2 dx from the corners, within the clearance. With the 2-D values a 3-D lattice got a particle at every cell centre (+77 % particles in 176 steps of a vortex at 24³).
  • Open boundaries (moving clouds): an interior particle that has left the region is removed with its content (the outflow), unless it crossed a wall (is_wall) of a shape-based domain, where it is put back onto the wall; and the insertion rule also holds between an interior particle and a ghost: the gap a cloud leaves when it recedes from a boundary (an inflow) is filled with seeds, whose content enters the region. No event is centred outside the region.
  • exclude_reduced_order: skip events near particles whose MOOD order was lowered in the last step (the shock band), where interpolation of any order is unreliable.
  • samples_per_dx: resolution of the remap quadrature lattice.
  • max_events: at most this many events per round.
  • max_rounds: a call repeats detection, remaps and neighbour search until no pair violates the rules, at most this many times. The events of one round are kept apart (below), a few per (9.5 dx)^2 in 2-D at R = 2.5 dx, and a pair can only be found while it is within the radius R: a gap between max_gap·dx and R that one round per step does not reach in time grows beyond R and is never filled (an expanding cloud with a small radius lost its stencils that way). 1 is the behaviour up to 0.9.9. A round is cheap (the neighbour lists are repaired next to its events, not searched again), so a smaller limit saves little: on a 3-D blast 3 instead of 8 rounds took the cost of a call from 0.32 to 0.26 time steps and the fewest neighbours of a particle from 37 to 7.
  • volume_estimator: the estimator the time stepper re-estimates the volumes with (a moving grid). After the topology change the volumes of the new cloud are estimated with it, and the change of Σ V U this causes is put back onto the particles the remap changed (bounded by their local extrema, like the remap's own repair), so the call is exact for the volumes the next steps use. nothing keeps the remap's own volumes (right for a static grid, whose volumes are never re-estimated).
  • At most one event per neighbourhood and per round. Event centres are kept (R + dx/2) √D + 3R/2 + 3dx/2 apart (see _event_spacing), so no event's quadrature sees a particle that another event removes or adds, and every remap knows what the others remove: the call is exactly conservative. Call once between time steps.

HyperCloudCore.adapt_particles! — Method
adapt_particles!(pg, ad::ParticleAdaptivity, max_order::Int; states = (), own_volumes = false)
    -> (; inserted, removed, mass_residual, escaped, held, seeded, refused, escaped_content, seeded_content)

Detects holes and clusters, applies the conservative remap for every event on the old topology, changes the particle set, and rebuilds the neighbour lists, in up to ad.max_rounds rounds. max_order is the scheme's maximum consistency order (new particles start there, and exclude_reduced_order compares against it). Requires valid neighbour lists and volumes (update_volumes!). mass_residual is the change of the interior content Σ V U (boundary particles excluded, which carry no volume) by the events of the call, which should be at round-off level. Merges and insertions involve interior particles only, and no event is centred outside the region; refused counts the events that could not be remapped and were left out (see _remap_event!).

On a moving cloud the call first removes the interior particles that left the region (escaped, with the content escaped_content; those that crossed a wall, is_wall, are put back onto it: held) and seeds the gaps between the cloud and the boundary (seeded, bringing seeded_content), see ParticleAdaptivity. The interior content changes by seeded_content - escaped_content + mass_residual over the call.

own_volumes: whether the volumes of the cloud are kept by the time stepper between its steps instead of being re-estimated after each (keeps_own_volumes: the content form of the Lagrangian movers). The remap's volumes then stay (the particles' shares of the partition, Σ V U exact without a correction), and ad.volume_estimator only gives the seeds their volume. false (the stepper re-estimates all volumes after every step, with the same estimator): all volumes are replaced, as the next step would, and a correction on the changed particles balances the whole change.

On a grid whose buffers reserve! froze, a CapacityError is thrown before the grid is changed by the seeds or by a round: the particle buffers and the neighbour slots the new particle set needs (_slots_after_events) are checked before the first remap of the round (earlier rounds stay applied, each of them conservative).

The event work (detection, remap, compaction) is serial and runs on the host. On a grid built with device_grid a kernel first checks whether any neighbour pair violates the spacing rules; only then is the grid copied to the host, adapted there, and copied back into the device grid in place (its arrays grow as needed), where the neighbour search runs. A well-spaced cloud costs one kernel and one reduction per call.

states are further state arrays over the cloud (the blocks of a block state: host arrays of the grid's state type that can grow, Vectors or Growables), remapped, compacted and extended exactly like pg.rhos (the geometric decisions are the same for all, the remap of every array is that of the single state); host grids only. mass_residual is that of pg.rhos.

HyperCloudCore.is_wall — Method
is_wall(bc::AbstractBoundaryCondition) -> Bool

Whether no flow crosses the boundary of the condition bc. The particle management of a moving cloud (adapt_particles!) puts an interior particle that crossed such a boundary back onto it (the crossing is the noise of its normal velocity) and removes one that crossed any other boundary with its content (an outflow). true for MirrorBC, false otherwise; define it for a condition type of your own that models a wall.
