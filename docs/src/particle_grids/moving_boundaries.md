# Moving Boundaries

A piece of the domain's shape can move: `Moving(shape, motion)` turns a shape into a rigid body whose pose at time $t$ is `motion(t)::RigidMotion` (translation $c$, rotation $R$, velocity $v$ and angular velocity $\Omega$; `translating(v)` and `rotating(ω; center)` build the common motions, any function returning a `RigidMotion` works). The region is still described by one shape, for example a channel with a rotating plate:

```julia
plate = Moving(Tagged(Cuboid((-0.2, -0.05), (0.2, 0.05)), t -> t + 4), rotating(1.0))
geom  = GeometricDomain(Cuboid((-1.0, -0.5), (1.0, 0.5); open = (true, false)) - plate;
                        is_periodic = (true, false), mins = (-1.0, -0.5), maxs = (1.0, 0.5),
                        bc_map = Dict(3 => wall, 4 => wall, 5 => wall, 6 => wall, 7 => wall, 8 => wall))
```

`at_time(shape, t)` is the shape with every moving piece at its pose at `t` (a static shape); `wall_velocity(shape, x, t)` the velocity of the piece nearest to `x`. The moving pieces are looked up by **tag**: every tag of a moving piece must belong to that piece alone (give it its own tags with `Tagged`; tags up to 63), and the grid records the piece of each tag when the particles are generated. A transformation of a moving piece goes inside `Moving` (`Moving(Translated(shape, offset), motion)`): a `Moving` under `Translated` or `Rotated` would move in the wrapper's frame and is refused. A motion in another float type than its shape (`rotating(1.0)` on a Float32 shape) is converted to the shape's type.

## How the cloud follows

The interior cloud stays where it is (Eulerian); only the ghost particles of a moving piece move, and the interior particles make way:

1. **Ghosts.** The ghost particles generated inside a piece at $t = 0$ are placed at the piece's pose at every stage time and at the end of every step (`place_ghosts!`): an exact rigid displacement of their positions and normals from the step start, so they never drift from the body. The time stepper runs its moving-grid path for this (position stages, a neighbour search per step) with grid velocity zero for the interior particles. The interior must stay static (`NoGridMover`): the volume estimators integrate the region at $t = 0$, so a grid mover together with moving pieces is refused, and so is a `volume_estimator` of the adaptivity. The grid is generated at the pieces' poses at $t = 0$; a step that starts at another time (a run from $t_0 > 0$, a restart) first places the ghosts and sweeps the cloud to its start time, in sub-steps over which no piece moves more than a quarter spacing.
2. **Boundary conditions** see the ghosts at the time of the state they build. The stored positions and normals belong to `pg.meta.boundary_time`; the fit-based conditions displace them to the stage time themselves (`tag_motion`), and the `WallPoint` their operator receives carries the wall velocity at the wall point. An operator for a moving wall reflects the velocity relative to the wall, `u_rel = u - w.velocity`.
3. **Sweep** (`sweep_boundaries!`, after every step), in two steps. First the **region**: on a lattice with two points per spacing, the points a piece has covered since the last sweep leave the interior particles that shared them, volume and content alike (the content there is the limited linear reconstruction of the nearest interior particle), and the points it has uncovered join them with the reconstructed state. The particle volumes follow the region exactly up to the quadrature (the interior volumes of a channel with a translating plate stay at the region's volume to 6 digits over 200 steps), the content of what was covered is gone and what was uncovered appears with the local state: a **consistent interpolation**, which is what a collocation scheme needs at a moving wall. The transport of the swept fluid is the equations' business, through the wall velocity in the conditions; enforcing the old content on the new region instead (a conservative remap) flattened the solution around every insertion in a growing region. Then the **topology**: interior particles inside a moving piece or closer than a quarter spacing to it are removed, lattice points of the canvas inside the region, within reach of a moving piece and at least half a spacing from it and from every remaining particle are inserted (on a static wall the lattice point lies on the wall, as in the initial cloud), each through the conservative remap of the [adaptivity](@ref "Particle Volumes and Adaptivity") on the new region (a removed particle's remaining share and content go to its neighbours, an inserted one takes its share from them). The removals run in rounds of independent remaps (centres at least the adaptivity's event spacing apart), each followed by the topology change and a neighbour search: all removals of a sweep at once, on the old topology, handed every remap the shares of all particles removed in its box, and a row of removals changed states by 0.2 and volumes by factors 0.5–2.8 up to 12 spacings from a 2-D face. The insertions follow in rounds of their own (all at once, every inserted particle took its share as if alone and the volumes swung between 0.2 and 2.7 h²). Particles whose whole share a fast piece covered are dropped first, without a remap (they have no volume and no content left). Last, where a piece uncovered the ghost band of a static wall (a piece receding along a wall), the wall's ghosts are added with the state of the nearest ghost of the same tag (a `FixedDirichlet` wall with data that vary along it gets the neighbour's value), and the static ghosts in the region take the wall geometry at the current time. `sweep_boundaries!` returns the counts and the content change of the call.

Keep the step small enough that a piece moves less than the clearance per step, $|w|\,\Delta t \le \Delta x/4$ (the sweep warns once when a particle was covered before it was removed). The time step estimate does not know the wall speed. Lattice points on or within half a spacing of a moving piece become its ghosts half a spacing outside instead of interior particles (a particle on a moving wall would be covered as soon as it moves; at a corner the push is repeated along the second face). A moving face must have ghosts of its own: a piston face on the end of the canvas shares the end's lattice row with the static wall there (the tie between the two pieces goes to the first), and the grid refuses such a domain. Extend the canvas beyond the face.

## What was measured

Scalar advection with velocity $(1, 0)$ in a channel periodic in $x$ and a plate $0.4 \times 0.1$ translating at $(1, 0)$: the fluid is at rest relative to the plate and the exact solution translates. With `MirrorBC(degree = 1)` on the plate and the walls the maximum error at $t = 0.5$ is 0.26 / 0.065 / 0.010 for $N = 20 / 40 / 80$ (a pulse that is constant at the plate), the solution stays within the initial range, the interior volumes sum to the region's volume (1.96) to six digits, the content changes by −0.0015 / −0.0009 / −0.0003, and the plate's ghosts sit on the exactly translated lattice. With data that vary at the plate ($1 + \tfrac12 \cos 5\pi(x - t)$, zero normal derivative on the plate's faces, so that the mirror condition is consistent) the error away from the plate (beyond 12 spacings) is 0.33 / 0.10 / 0.027, next to the plate 0.34 / 0.20 / 0.15 (all events of a sweep at once: 0.36 / 0.46 / 1.48, diverging). A plane piston advancing into a fluid at rest with smooth data keeps the solution within 0.031 / 0.027 / 0.014 of the data, a ball moving through a 3-D box keeps it within $[0.9953, 2.0047]$ for data in $[1, 2]$ (at once: $[0.875, 2.125]$), and a piston receding in a channel periodic across its face inserts every lattice point once (the seam used to be inserted twice). The same in 1-D (a piston receding at the flow speed, the fluid at rest relative to it): 0.19 / 0.060 / 0.014 for $N = 50 / 100 / 200$, the interior volumes sum to the tube's length exactly, and the content grows by the inflow (0.51 / 0.502 / 0.501 against 0.5). A piston advancing into a fluid at rest removes the covered particles with their content, keeps the first particle between a quarter and one spacing from the face and the volumes at the tube's length. A plate rotating in a fluid at rest keeps the scalar within its initial range and the volumes constant (the sweep only interpolates; the peak the plate sweeps through erodes). Ghost positions after 200 steps of a rotation agree with the exact rotation to $2 \cdot 10^{-15}$.

## Limits

- Rigid motions prescribed in time; nothing is coupled back (no fluid–structure interaction).
- The ghost band of a piece is the one generated at $t = 0$ inside it: a thin piece has few ghost rows, and a piece that approaches a wall or another piece keeps its own normals and wall distances (the composite geometry is not re-evaluated for the ghosts).
- The interior particles' stored wall distances and normals are not updated as a piece moves; only the conditions on the ghosts read them.
- The volume of the region at $t = 0$ (`domain_volume`) is what the volume estimators renormalise to; the stepper does not re-estimate volumes while only pieces move, so the sweep's volumes stand. Grid movers and the adaptivity's volume estimators are refused together with moving pieces.
- Pieces have no periodic images: a piece with points on a periodic end (sampled at half a spacing) is refused at the sweep.
- Near a face that moves with the flow the convergence is slow: a 2-D piston receding at the flow speed with `MirrorBC(degree = 1)` on the face (data with a normal derivative there) gives 0.12 / 0.15 / 0.14 / 0.081 for $N = 20 / 40 / 80 / 160$ (1-D: 0.24 / 0.19 / 0.12 / 0.079), and with `ExtrapolationBC(1)` on such a face the error grows with the resolution (1-D 0.12 / 0.29 / 0.44 / 0.93, the same before the sweep's rounds).
- Behind a piston receding at the flow speed along walls (data consistent with every condition), the uncovered part has errors 0.072 / 0.048 / 0.061 / 0.063 for $N = 20 / 40 / 80 / 160$: it does **not converge**, and the cause is open. The remap gives inserted particles volumes that differ from a generated cloud's (wall-row particles 0–0.3 h², inner ones 0.5–1.1 h², against 1.0); the parent's one-round insertion converged here only through its volume errors, which spoiled other cases.
- Static ghosts are never removed: a piece that moves back over a wall's band keeps the static ghosts it covers inside it (no interior particle reads them).
- `mass_residual` is the content change of the region pass and the remaps; the region pass clamps the new states to their local bounds, which is not conservative.
- 3-D: a sweep that removes a row costs seconds (N = 24, 16k particles: about 16 s, 1.5× the cost before the rounds), mostly the per-event remap (local sets of radius 12 h); 3-D moving boundaries are not practical yet.
- Cost: a step with a moving piece is about 5× a static one (channel with the plate, 13.8k particles, 2 threads: 26 ms against 5.5 ms, 13 MB allocated). The sweep takes 3.4 ms (rounds and neighbour searches when particles are removed or inserted, 0.2 ms otherwise); most of the rest is the moving-grid path, which recomputes the geometry of every particle each stage although only ghosts move.
- `sweep_boundaries!` is a host pass (a device grid is swept on a host copy), like the adaptivity.
