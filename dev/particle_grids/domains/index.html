Geometric Domains

A GeometricDomain describes the physical problem region and nothing else: its bounding box, which points are inside, how boundary points are tagged, which axes are periodic, and which boundary condition belongs to which tag. Numerical padding (ghost layers) and point generation are handled separately when the ParticleGrid is built.

Shapes

The region is described by a signed distance $\varphi(x)$: negative inside, zero on the boundary, positive outside, $|\varphi|$ the distance to the nearest boundary point. Everything the boundary treatment needs follows from it: the outward unit normal $n = \nabla\varphi$, the nearest wall point $x - \varphi n$ and the mirror image $x - 2\varphi n$. The solver only ever evaluates the functions of the shape interface, so a region sampled from a CAD model (a level set on a grid, from a point-in-solid classifier and a distance query of the CAD kernel, or from a surface mesh) only needs a subtype that implements them.

The active piece of a union is the one with the smallest signed distance (the nearest piece for an outside point), of an intersection the one with the largest (the piece the point is farthest outside of). Its normal and tag are used, so a ghost particle outside a channel with an obstacle is tagged by the wall or the obstacle face it actually lies behind. The signed distance of a cuboid is the Euclidean distance also outside its edges and corners, where the normal points to the corner and the mirror image is the point reflection through it.

# A 2-D channel with a step (the Mach 3 step): walls 1-4 from the canvas, the step faces 5 (front) and 8 (top)
shape = Cuboid((0.0, 0.0), (3.0, 1.0)) - Tagged(Cuboid((0.6, -1.0), (4.0, 0.2)), t -> t + 4)
geom  = GeometricDomain(shape; bc_map = Dict(1 => FixedDirichlet(), 2 => OutflowBC(),
                                             3 => wall, 4 => wall, 5 => wall, 8 => wall))

# A rotated plate in a periodic channel: the canvas is open in x, so its box must be given
plate = Rotated(Cuboid((-0.2, -0.02), (0.2, 0.02)), π / 6)
geom  = GeometricDomain(Cuboid((-1.0, -0.5), (1.0, 0.5); open = (true, false)) - Tagged(plate, 5);
                        is_periodic = (true, false), mins = (-1.0, -0.5), maxs = (1.0, 0.5), bc_map = ...)

GeometricDomain(shape; bc_map, is_periodic, mins, maxs, label, volume) takes the bounding box from the shape; along an axis where the shape is unbounded (open cuboid axes, half spaces) mins/maxs must be given; on bounded axes they may enlarge the box (the lattice is anchored at it), but a box that would crop the shape is an error (intersect the shape with a Cuboid to cut it, so that the cut carries ghosts and a tag; the bounding boxes of rotated and combined shapes are conservative). A shape used on a periodic axis must be open along it: the ends of a periodic axis are no boundary. The volume of the region within the box is computed once, by the constructor: analytic for cuboids and balls (and their tagged, moved copies), a lattice integration of the signed distance otherwise (shape_volume, 10–50 ms); pass volume if it is known. domain_volume returns it. Tags must be positive (0 marks interior points); Tagged and the domain's tag function refuse others. A ball decides inside/outside on the squared distance, as the spherical domain did before shapes (the points within $\Delta x/2$ outside it are then snapped onto it, see Point generation).

A shape of your own subtypes AbstractShape{D, T} and implements signed_distance and bounding_box; boundary_normal falls back to central differences of the signed distance, boundary_tag to 1, shape_volume to the lattice integration. (Up to 0.8 a domain held only an interior test and a tag function; that constructor still exists, see below.)

Wall geometry per particle

Every particle of a grid built on a shape-based domain stores the outward unit normal of its nearest wall and its signed wall distance (wall_geometry; pg.core.normals, pg.core.wall_distances, positive for ghosts), filled when the grid is built and when adaptivity inserts particles. Behind a convex edge or corner of the ghost band (outside two faces) a ghost stores the face its tag names instead, the one it is farther outside of: that face's normal and the distance to its plane, so that it is reflected in its own face (with the diagonal normal to the corner, a slip wall's operator turned the velocity along the wall into the wall where the wall meets an inflow, and a uniform flow did not survive the corner). The conditions that build ghost states from the interior use them: MirrorBC evaluates the interior at the mirror image $x - 2\varphi n$, and its operator receives the wall point, normal and distance as a WallPoint. The grid also stores the wall's curvature (pg.core.wall_curvatures: the Hessian of the signed distance at the wall point, in a tangent basis of the normal), which the operator receives as WallPoint.hessian: analytic for balls (zero for boxes and half spaces), passed through complements, transformations and moving pieces, by finite differences of signed_distance for other shapes (zero across kinks, the corners of polygons, where three step sizes disagree, and where the radius of curvature is below ten finite-difference steps; in Float32 that loses the curvature of small user circles at some wall points). A domain built from functions stores NaNs, and a condition that needs the wall geometry (requires_shape) is refused by the ParticleGrid constructor on such a domain. Behind a convex corner where two pieces meet (a step, a wedge, a ramp) the nearest wall point of a ghost is the corner itself, found by projection and a descent along the boundary (exact to round-off for corners of about 15° and more; below that the projections converge too slowly: 14 % off at 5°). Its mirror image is the point reflection through the corner, which lies inside the region for corners of 90° or more; behind sharper corners it can lie outside, where the fit of a MirrorBC extrapolates.

Built-in shapes

Rectangular

get_rectangular_domain(T, mins, maxs; bc_map = Dict(), is_periodic = false)

The Cuboid mins ≤ x ≤ maxs, open along the periodic axes. is_periodic is either one Bool for all axes or an NTuple{D,Bool}. Boundary points get the tag of the face they lie outside of: 2d - 1 for the lower and 2d for the upper face of axis d; outside several faces (the edges and corners of the ghost band) the one they are farthest outside of, the lower tag on ties. The faces of a periodic axis are ignored. (Up to 0.7.9 the 2-D tag was that of the nearest face plane, also measured from inside the box, so ghosts outside one wall close to another plane carried the other wall's tag.)

Dtags
11 left (x = min), 2 right
21 left, 2 right, 3 bottom, 4 top (a channel periodic in x has only 3 and 4)
31/2 at x = min/max, 3/4 at y, 5/6 at z

Up to version 0.7.9, 1-D and 3-D boxes tagged every boundary point 1, so Dict(1 => bc) covered both walls of an interval (and every face of a box) and a tag-2 entry was dead. Such a map now covers only the left wall (the x-min face), and the constructor warns that the other tags have no condition: write Dict(1 => bc, 2 => bc) (Dict(t => bc for t in 1:6) in 3-D). For FixedDirichlet that is the same as before. For OutflowBC in 3-D it is not exactly the same at the edges and corners of the ghost band: each tag is resolved on its own, and a ghost takes its donor from the interior and from the already resolved ghosts of its own tag only (2–5 % of the ghosts of a cube get different states than with one tag for all faces).

Boundary particles whose tag has no entry in bc_map keep their state (like a Dirichlet condition), and an entry whose tag no particle carries does nothing. When a non-empty bc_map and the tags of the boundary particles do not match, the ParticleGrid constructor warns (once per combination and session). An empty bc_map freezes every boundary particle without a warning.

Spherical

get_spherical_domain(T, center, radius; bc_map = Dict(), is_periodic = false)

A disc (2-D) or ball (3-D), the Ball of center and radius; every exterior point gets tag 1.

From functions

GeometricDomain(label, mins, maxs, is_periodic, is_interior, get_tag, bc_map)

A domain from an interior test and a tag function, without a shape. It generates the same particles and tags as before 0.9, but no wall normals (NaN), so it serves FixedDirichlet, OutflowBC, ExtrapolationBC and user conditions that do not read them; a MirrorBC is refused. A domain_volume method for its label is needed as well.

Point generation

When a ParticleGrid is constructed, the domain is wrapped in a ComputationalDomain, and points are generated as follows:

  1. Canvas. On non-periodic axes the bounding box is extended by $\lceil R/\Delta x_d\rceil$ spacings of that axis on each side to host ghost particles, i.e. at least $R$ on every axis; periodic axes get no padding. (Up to version 0.7.9 every axis got $\lceil R/\Delta x_{\max}\rceil$ spacings, which left the band thinner than $R$ on the finer axis of an anisotropic grid.)
  2. Lattice. A Cartesian lattice with spacing nominal_dx (plus optional jitter randomness) covers the canvas. A non-periodic axis gets round(L/dx) + 1 points, endpoints included: if a wall lies on the lattice (its index (w - canvas_min)/dx is an integer up to the round-off of the wall, the canvas minimum and the division), that lattice point is put on the wall exactly before the jitter is added, so both walls are interior. A wall between two lattice points (an extent that is not a multiple of dx) gets no lattice index, however close; the point less than dx/2 outside it is snapped onto it in step 3. (Up to version 0.7.9 a distance tolerance did the snapping; far from the origin in Float32 it grew to almost dx and could put two points on a wall.) A periodic axis gets round(L/dx) points, because the endpoint coincides with the start point after wrapping.
  3. No ghosts close to the wall. A jittered point whose lattice position is interior but which the jitter carried outside (half of a jittered row on a wall) is reflected back inside, to its mirror image $x - 2\varphi n$ (its lattice position without a shape, or if the mirror image is not interior). On a shape-based domain every other point less than $\Delta x_{\min}/2$ outside a wall is snapped onto its nearest wall point and becomes interior, as the lattice row on a box face does: then every ghost is at least $\Delta x_{\min}/2$ outside and the interior reaches the wall. On an unjittered lattice a snapped point stays at least $\Delta x/2$ from every other lattice point; a snapped point that would come within $\Delta x/4$ of an interior particle (a jittered or reflected one next to the wall, another snapped point behind a corner; minimum images across periodic seams) is pushed out to $\Delta x/2$ as a ghost instead. (Particle adaptivity may later merge a snapped particle with a close neighbour, which moves it off the wall; it never creates ghosts, so the $\Delta x/2$ rule for ghosts holds.) Ghosts close to the wall spoil the fit-based conditions (Stability). Grids whose walls lie on the lattice and are not jittered are unchanged; jittered ones and walls off the lattice (balls, obstacles, extents that are not a multiple of dx) differ from earlier versions in the points that crossed a wall or lay within $\Delta x/2$ outside it (the random numbers are drawn in the same order).
  4. Narrow band. Lattice points outside the physical region are kept only if they lie within $R + \Delta x_{\max}$ of an interior point. These become the boundary (ghost) particles.
  5. Tags and wall geometry. get_tag sets tags and is_boundary = (tag != 0); wall_geometry fills the normals and wall distances.

Boundary particles carry the boundary condition and are never updated by the spatial scheme; see Boundary Conditions.

HyperCloudCore.GeometricDomain — Type
GeometricDomain{GEO, D, T, F_Interior, F_Tag, S, P}
GeometricDomain(shape::AbstractShape; bc_map, is_periodic = false, mins = nothing, maxs = nothing, label = Val(:shape))
GeometricDomain(label, mins, maxs, is_periodic, is_interior, get_tag, bc_map)

The physical region of a problem and its boundary pieces: the bounding box, which points are inside, how boundary points are tagged, which axes are periodic, and which boundary condition belongs to which tag.

The first constructor describes the region by a signed-distance AbstractShape (built-in boxes, balls and half spaces, their unions, intersections and differences, translated and rotated copies). Points with signed_distance(shape, x) <= 0 are interior; the others get boundary_tag(shape, x). Every particle of a grid built on such a domain stores its outward wall normal and signed wall distance (pg.core.normals, pg.core.wall_distances), which the higher-order conditions ExtrapolationBC and MirrorBC use. The bounding box is that of the shape; along an axis where it is unbounded (open box axes, i.e. periodic axes, or shapes cut from half spaces) mins/maxs must be given; on bounded axes they may enlarge the box (the lattice is anchored at it), but a box that would crop the shape is an error (intersect the shape with a Cuboid instead; the bounding boxes of rotated and combined shapes are conservative). A shape used on a periodic axis must be open along it (its ends are no boundary; Cuboid(...; open = ...)). The volume of the region (the shape within the box; domain_volume) is computed once here, analytically where the shape allows it and by shape_volume otherwise; pass volume if it is known.

The second constructor takes the interior test is_interior(x) and tag function get_tag(x) directly (no shape; shape === nothing). Such a domain has no wall normals (NaN), so conditions that need them refuse it at grid construction.

Fields

  • label::GEO: The geometric identifier (e.g., Val(:rectangular), Val(:spherical), Val(:shape)), used to dispatch domain_volume.
  • mins::Space{D, T}: The minimal bounding box coordinates.
  • maxs::Space{D, T}: The maximal bounding box coordinates.
  • is_periodic::SVector{D, Bool}: Periodic boundary flags for each spatial dimension.
  • is_interior::F_Interior: A function evaluating whether a given coordinate resides within the geometry.
  • get_tag::F_Tag: A function generating integer boundary tags based on spatial location (0 inside).
  • bc_map::Dict{Int, AbstractBoundaryCondition}: A mapping of integer tags to specific boundary condition evaluators.
  • shape::S: The AbstractShape of the region, or nothing.
  • volume::T: The volume of the region within the box at t = 0 (NaN without a shape).
  • pieces::P: The Moving pieces of the shape (a tuple, empty for a static region).
  • piece_of_tag::Vector{Int}: For every boundary tag (index tag + 1) the index of the moving piece it belongs to in pieces, 0 for a static piece; filled when the particles are generated.

With moving pieces is_interior(x, t), get_tag(x, t) and wall_geometry(geom, x, t) evaluate the shape at time t (at_time); the one-argument forms are t = 0.

HyperCloudCore.get_rectangular_domain — Method
get_rectangular_domain(::Type{T}, mins, maxs; bc_map, is_periodic = false)

Generates a rectangular GeometricDomain instance (the Cuboid mins ≤ x ≤ maxs, open along the periodic axes).

  • Tags every boundary point with the face it lies outside of: 2d - 1 for the lower and 2d for the upper face of axis d, i.e. 1-D: 1 left, 2 right; 2-D: 1 left, 2 right, 3 bottom, 4 top; 3-D: 1/2 at x = min/max, 3/4 at y, 5/6 at z.   • The ParticleGrid constructor warns when bc_map names a tag no boundary particle carries, or when boundary particles carry a tag bc_map does not cover (they then keep their state, like a Dirichlet condition).

HyperCloudCore.wall_geometry — Method
wall_geometry(geom::GeometricDomain, x) -> (normal, distance)

Outward unit wall normal and signed wall distance (negative inside the region) of the point x, from the domain's shape; NaNs for a domain without a shape. Stored per particle in pg.core.normals and pg.core.wall_distances when a grid is built or particles are inserted, except behind a convex edge or corner (outside two faces), where a ghost stores the face its tag names instead: that face's normal and the distance to its plane (see MirrorBC).

The signed distance of a combined shape (max/min of its pieces) is only a bound where several pieces are active at once: behind a corner formed by two pieces (outside the floor and inside the step of a channel, or outside both faces of a wedge) the nearest piece is not the nearest point of the region. The wall point is therefore found in two stages: projecting onto the active piece and repeating from there until the projection lies on the boundary (alternating projections, which reach a boundary point but slowly at acute corners and not necessarily the nearest one at obtuse corners), then moving along the boundary towards x (the tangential part of x - p, projected back onto the boundary, with step halving) while that gets closer, which ends at the nearest point of a face or at the corner. The normal is the direction from that point to x and the distance its length. For a point whose first projection already lies on the boundary (every point of a primitive shape, most points of a combination) the shape's own normal and distance are returned unchanged. Inside a combination the distance is the combined signed distance (a bound for unions; no condition reads interior wall data).

HyperCloudCore.AbstractShape — Type
AbstractShape{D, T}

A region of D-dimensional space with element type T, described by its signed distance (negative inside, positive outside). The physical region of a GeometricDomain is a shape; ghost particles lie outside it.

API

  • signed_distance(shape, x) -> T (required).
  • bounding_box(shape) -> (mins, maxs) (required for the region of a domain; may be infinite for pieces that are intersected with a bounded one).
  • boundary_normal(shape, x) -> Space{D, T}: unit gradient of the signed distance, i.e. the outward normal of the nearest boundary point. Defaults to central differences of signed_distance.
  • boundary_tag(shape, x) -> Int: the tag of the boundary piece nearest to x (a positive integer, used for points outside the shape). Defaults to 1.
  • shape_volume(shape) -> T: the volume of the region. Defaults to a lattice integration over the bounding box.

Built-in shapes: Cuboid, Ball, HalfSpace; combinations with union, intersect, setdiff (or -) and complement; Translated and Rotated copies; Tagged renumbers the boundary tags of a piece.

HyperCloudCore.Cuboid — Type
Cuboid(mins, maxs; open = false)

The axis-aligned box mins ≤ x ≤ maxs. Its signed distance is exact (Euclidean outside the edges and corners). The faces are tagged 2d - 1 (lower) and 2d (upper) for axis d; outside several faces the one the point is farthest outside of decides, the lower tag on ties.

open (one Bool or one per axis) drops the faces of an axis: the box is unbounded along it, which is how a domain represents a periodic axis (its ends are no boundary).

HyperCloudCore.Moving — Type
Moving(shape, motion)

shape moved rigidly by motion(t)::RigidMotion (the body frame is the frame shape is described in; at t = 0 the motion should be the identity). A domain built from a shape with moving pieces moves their ghost particles with them every stage, removes interior particles they cover and refills the region they leave (see the docs on moving boundaries). The static methods (signed_distance(m, x) etc.) evaluate the piece at t = 0; at_time gives the shape at a time. A moving piece must not contain another one. A motion in another float type than the shape (rotating(1.0) on a Float32 shape) is converted to the shape's type.

HyperCloudCore.RigidMotion — Type
RigidMotion(c, R, v, Ω)

The pose and velocity of a rigid body at one instant: a point y of the body (in the body's own frame, the frame its shape is described in) is at c + R y, the body's velocity at the world point x is v + Ω (x - c) (v = dc/dt, Ω = dR/dt Rᵀ skew). RigidMotion(D, T) is the identity (at rest). The motion of a Moving piece is a function t -> RigidMotion; translating and rotating build the common ones.

HyperCloudCore.shape_volume — Method
shape_volume(shape; samples = 2_000_000) -> T

Volume of the region. Analytic for Cuboid, Ball and their translated, rotated and tagged copies. Every other shape is integrated on a lattice of about samples cubic cells over its bounding box, each cell counted with the volume fraction clamp(1/2 - φ/h, 0, 1) of its centre's signed distance (exact for a wall parallel to a cell face, second order otherwise: relative errors below 1e-5 in 2-D and 1e-3 in 3-D at the default for piecewise smooth boundaries). Define a method for shapes whose volume is known.
