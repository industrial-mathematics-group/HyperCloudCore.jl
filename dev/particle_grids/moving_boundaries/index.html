Moving Boundaries

A piece of the domain's shape can move: Moving(shape, motion) turns a shape into a rigid body whose pose at time $t$ is motion(t)::RigidMotion (translation $c$, rotation $R$, velocity $v$ and angular velocity $\Omega$; translating(v) and rotating(ω; center) build the common motions, any function returning a RigidMotion works). The region is still described by one shape, for example a channel with a rotating plate:

plate = Moving(Tagged(Cuboid((-0.2, -0.05), (0.2, 0.05)), t -> t + 4), rotating(1.0))
geom  = GeometricDomain(Cuboid((-1.0, -0.5), (1.0, 0.5); open = (true, false)) - plate;
                        is_periodic = (true, false), mins = (-1.0, -0.5), maxs = (1.0, 0.5),
                        bc_map = Dict(3 => wall, 4 => wall, 5 => wall, 6 => wall, 7 => wall, 8 => wall))

at_time(shape, t) is the shape with every moving piece at its pose at t (a static shape); wall_velocity(shape, x, t) and wall_acceleration(shape, x, t) the velocity and acceleration of the piece nearest to x (the acceleration by central differences of the pose). The moving pieces are looked up by tag: every tag of a moving piece must belong to that piece alone (give it its own tags with Tagged; tags up to 63), and the grid records the piece of each tag when the particles are generated. A transformation of a moving piece goes inside Moving (Moving(Translated(shape, offset), motion)): a Moving under Translated or Rotated would move in the wrapper's frame and is refused. A motion in another float type than its shape (rotating(1.0) on a Float32 shape) is converted to the shape's type.

How the cloud follows

The interior cloud stays where it is (Eulerian); only the ghost particles of a moving piece move, and the interior particles make way:

  1. Ghosts. The ghost particles generated inside a piece at $t = 0$ are placed at the piece's pose at every stage time and at the end of every step (place_ghosts!): an exact rigid displacement of their positions and normals from the step start, so they never drift from the body. The time stepper runs its moving-grid path for this (position stages, a neighbour search per step) with grid velocity zero for the interior particles. The interior must stay static (NoGridMover): the volume estimators integrate the region at $t = 0$, so a grid mover together with moving pieces is refused, and so is a volume_estimator of the adaptivity. The grid is generated at the pieces' poses at $t = 0$; a step that starts at another time (a run from $t_0 > 0$, a restart) first places the ghosts and sweeps the cloud to its start time, in sub-steps over which no piece moves more than a quarter spacing.
  2. Boundary conditions see the ghosts at the time of the state they build. The stored positions and normals belong to pg.meta.boundary_time; the fit-based conditions displace them to the stage time themselves (tag_motion), and the WallPoint their operator receives carries the wall velocity and acceleration at the wall point, the piece's spin Ω and the wall's curvature. An operator for a moving wall reflects the velocity relative to the wall, u_rel = u - w.velocity, and can give the mirrored pressure the normal gradient of the fluid's momentum along the wall, ∂p/∂n = -ρ (n ⋅ a + 2 n ⋅ Ω u_rel - u_t ⋅ H u_t) (u_t the tangential part of u_rel, H = w.hessian; HyperCloud's SlipWall uses it). The material acceleration alone is wrong for a rotating wall: a disc spinning about its centre has a centripetal a but a surface that does not move, and the term stirred a gas at rest (1 % in the density); the spin and curvature terms cancel it.
  3. Sweep (sweep_boundaries!, after every step), in two steps, neither of which touches the state of a particle that stays: the states are point values of a collocation scheme, and rewriting them as the volume-weighted blend with the reconstructed content of the swept points (a cell-average operation, O(h) per event for every particle within $R$ of a face, at every step) was amplified by the scheme into an error behind a receding face that did not converge (0.06 for $N = 40$ to 160 in the case below; 0.03 / 0.018 / 0.018 without it). First the region: on a lattice with two points per spacing, the points a piece has covered since the last sweep leave the volumes of the interior particles that shared them, and the points it has uncovered join them. The particle volumes follow the region exactly up to the quadrature (the interior volumes of a channel with a translating plate stay at the region's volume to 6 digits over 200 steps). Then the topology: interior particles inside a moving piece or closer than a quarter spacing to it are removed, lattice points of the canvas inside the region, within reach of a moving piece and at least half a spacing from it and from every remaining particle are inserted (on a static wall the lattice point lies on the wall, as in the initial cloud), each through the remap of the adaptivity on the new region in its point-value mode: a removed particle's remaining share goes to its neighbours, an inserted one takes its share from them and its state is the limited linear reconstruction of its neighbourhood averaged over that share, clipped to the old states within $R$. The content of what was covered is gone and what was uncovered appears with the local state: an interpolation, which is what a collocation scheme needs at a moving wall. The transport of the swept fluid is the equations' business, through the wall velocity in the conditions; enforcing the old content on the new region instead (a conservative remap) flattened the solution around every insertion in a growing region. The removals run in rounds of independent remaps (centres at least the adaptivity's event spacing apart), each followed by the topology change and a neighbour search: all removals of a sweep at once, on the old topology, handed every remap the shares of all particles removed in its box, and a row of removals changed volumes by factors 0.5–2.8 up to 12 spacings from a 2-D face. The insertions follow in rounds of their own (all at once, every inserted particle took its share as if alone and the volumes swung between 0.2 and 2.7 h²). Particles whose whole share a fast piece covered are dropped first, without a remap (they have no volume left). Last, where a piece uncovered the ghost band of a static wall (a piece receding along a wall), the wall's ghosts are added with the state of the nearest ghost of the same tag (a FixedDirichlet wall with data that vary along it gets the neighbour's value), and the static ghosts in the region take the wall geometry at the current time. sweep_boundaries! returns the counts and the content change of the call.

Keep the step small enough that a piece moves less than the clearance per step, $|w|\,\Delta t \le \Delta x/4$ (the sweep warns once when a particle was covered before it was removed). The time step estimate does not know the wall speed. Lattice points on or within half a spacing of a moving piece become its ghosts half a spacing outside instead of interior particles (a particle on a moving wall would be covered as soon as it moves; at a corner the push is repeated along the second face). A moving face must have ghosts of its own: a piston face on the end of the canvas shares the end's lattice row with the static wall there (the tie between the two pieces goes to the first), and the grid refuses such a domain. Extend the canvas beyond the face.

What was measured

Scalar advection with velocity $(1, 0)$ in a channel periodic in $x$ and a plate $0.4 \times 0.1$ translating at $(1, 0)$: the fluid is at rest relative to the plate and the exact solution translates. With MirrorBC(degree = 1) on the plate and the walls the maximum error at $t = 0.5$ is 0.26 / 0.065 / 0.010 for $N = 20 / 40 / 80$ (a pulse that is constant at the plate), the solution stays within the initial range, the interior volumes sum to the region's volume (1.96) to six digits, the content changes by −0.0015 / −0.0009 / −0.0003, and the plate's ghosts sit on the exactly translated lattice. With data that vary at the plate ($1 + \tfrac12 \cos 5\pi(x - t)$, zero normal derivative on the plate's faces, so that the mirror condition is consistent) the error away from the plate (beyond 12 spacings) is 0.33 / 0.10 / 0.027, next to the plate 0.35 / 0.18 / 0.065 (with the sweep rewriting the states of the particles that stay, as a conservative remap does: 0.34 / 0.20 / 0.15; all events of a sweep at once: 0.36 / 0.46 / 1.48, diverging). A plane piston advancing into a fluid at rest with smooth data leaves the data untouched (the removals do not write the particles that stay; the conservative remap kept them within 0.031 / 0.027 / 0.014), a ball moving through a 3-D box keeps it within $[0.9953, 2.0047]$ for data in $[1, 2]$ (at once: $[0.875, 2.125]$), and a piston receding in a channel periodic across its face inserts every lattice point once (the seam used to be inserted twice). The same in 1-D (a piston receding at the flow speed, the fluid at rest relative to it): 0.19 / 0.060 / 0.014 for $N = 50 / 100 / 200$, the interior volumes sum to the tube's length exactly, and the content grows by the inflow (0.504 / 0.5005 / 0.5002 against 0.5). A piston advancing into a fluid at rest removes the covered particles with their content, keeps the first particle between a quarter and one spacing from the face and the volumes at the tube's length. A plate rotating in a fluid at rest keeps the scalar within its initial range and the volumes constant (the sweep only interpolates; the peak the plate sweeps through erodes). Ghost positions after 200 steps of a rotation agree with the exact rotation to $2 \cdot 10^{-15}$.

Limits

  • Rigid motions prescribed in time; nothing is coupled back (no fluid–structure interaction).
  • The ghost band of a piece is the one generated at $t = 0$ inside it: a thin piece has few ghost rows, and a piece that approaches a wall or another piece keeps its own normals and wall distances (the composite geometry is not re-evaluated for the ghosts).
  • The interior particles' stored wall distances and normals are not updated as a piece moves; only the conditions on the ghosts read them.
  • The volume of the region at $t = 0$ (domain_volume) is what the volume estimators renormalise to; the stepper does not re-estimate volumes while only pieces move, so the sweep's volumes stand. Grid movers and the adaptivity's volume estimators are refused together with moving pieces.
  • Pieces have no periodic images: a piece with points on a periodic end (sampled at half a spacing) is refused at the sweep.
  • At a face that moves with the flow the error is first order (order 0.6–0.8 in the maximum) at the particles next to the face and grows linearly in time: the truncation error of the stencils there, which hold the moving ghosts, is stationary in the frame of the face (a degree-2 mirror fit changes nothing: 0.092 / 0.055 / 0.039 against 0.088 / 0.053 / 0.038 below). A 2-D piston receding at the flow speed along walls with data consistent with every condition (MirrorBC(degree = 1) on the face): 0.088 / 0.053 / 0.038 for $N = 40 / 80 / 160$ next to the face and 0.030 / 0.018 / 0.018 three to six spacings behind it, where the sweep that rewrote the states of the particles that stay gave 0.055 / 0.061 / 0.070 (non-convergent). Data with a normal derivative at the mirror face: 0.14 / 0.11 / 0.081 / 0.052 for $N = 20 / 40 / 80 / 160$ (before 0.12 / 0.15 / 0.14 / 0.081). In 1-D with a sine, a mirror face gives 0.14 / 0.087 / 0.053 / 0.032 and an ExtrapolationBC(1) face 0.12 / 0.089 / 0.053 / 0.033 for $N = 50 / 100 / 200 / 400$ (before 0.24 / 0.19 / 0.12 / 0.079, and 0.12 / 0.29 / 0.44 / 0.93, diverging).
  • The inserted particles' volumes differ from a generated cloud's (0.59–1.28 h² in the uncovered part behind the 2-D piston, wall rows included). The volumes do not enter the scheme (they are the remap's partition and the conservation diagnostics), and interior_mass follows the region's content to the quadrature; a local re-estimate for inserted particles is possible but would break the exact volume balance of the sweep.
  • Static ghosts are never removed: a piece that moves back over a wall's band keeps the static ghosts it covers inside it (no interior particle reads them). They are kept on purpose: a FixedDirichlet wall's data live in its ghosts, and ghosts added behind a receding piece can only copy a neighbour's value.
  • mass_residual is the content change of the sweep (the volumes follow the region, the states of the particles that stay do not change), which is not conservative: the content of the covered part is lost, the uncovered part appears with the local state.
  • 3-D: a sweep that removes a row costs seconds (N = 24, 16k particles: 4.8 s and 0.9 GB allocated, down from 13 s and 3 GB when the remap integrated over a box of half-width 2R around every event instead of R; 2-D rows: 50 ms), mostly the per-event remap; 3-D moving boundaries are not practical yet.
  • Cost: a step with a moving piece is about 5× a static one (channel with the plate, 13.8k particles, 2 threads: 26 ms against 5.5 ms, 13 MB allocated). The sweep takes 3.4 ms (rounds and neighbour searches when particles are removed or inserted, 0.2 ms otherwise); most of the rest is the moving-grid path, which recomputes the geometry of every particle each stage although only ghosts move.
  • sweep_boundaries! is a host pass (a device grid is swept on a host copy), like the adaptivity.

Reference

HyperCloudCore.place_ghosts! — Method
place_ghosts!(pg, X, t_from, t_to)

Moves the ghost particles of the moving pieces in the position buffer X (their positions at t_from) to the pieces' poses at t_to, rotates their stored normals and wall curvatures from pg.meta.boundary_time to t_to and sets boundary_time = t_to. Exact rigid motion (no integration). A kernel; nothing happens on a domain without moving pieces.

HyperCloudCore.sweep_boundaries! — Method
sweep_boundaries!(pg, t_old, t_new, max_order; clearance = 0.25, min_gap = 0.5, samples_per_dx = 2)
    -> (; inserted, removed, mass_residual)

Makes the interior cloud follow the moving pieces of the domain from their poses at the last sweep (pg.meta.sweep_time; the argument t_old is not used) to those at t_new (their ghosts must already be at t_new: place_ghosts!, or the time stepper, which calls this after every step). Interior particles that a piece now covers, or that lie closer than clearance spacings to it, are removed; lattice points of the canvas that are inside the region, at least min_gap spacings from every moving piece and from every remaining particle are inserted (the lattice points of a static wall lie on the wall, as in the initial cloud).

Two steps, and the interior particles' states are point values that neither touches: a particle that stays keeps its state (the sweep rewrote it, as the volume-weighted blend with the reconstructed content of the swept points, and the scheme amplified the perturbations into an error behind a receding face that did not converge). First the region: on a lattice with samples_per_dx points per spacing, the points a piece has covered since the last sweep (pg.meta.sweep_time) leave the volumes of the interior particles that shared them and the points it has uncovered join them, so that the particle volumes follow the region (the interior volumes sum to its volume up to the quadrature). Then the topology: each removal and insertion is the remap of the adaptivity on the new region (_remap_event! with point_values = true): a removed particle's remaining share of the region goes to its neighbours, an inserted particle takes its share from them and its state is the limited linear reconstruction of its neighbourhood averaged over that share, clipped to the old states within R; the states of the others stay. The content of what a piece covered is gone, what it uncovered appears with the local state: an interpolation, as a collocation scheme needs at a moving wall; the transport of the swept fluid is the equations' business, through the wall velocity in the WallPoint of the conditions (enforcing the old content on the new region, a conservative remap, flattened the solution around every insertion). The removals run in rounds of independent events (centres at least the adaptivity's event spacing apart, _event_spacing), each round followed by the topology change and a neighbour search, so that every remap sees the particles the earlier ones removed; the insertions follow in rounds of their own. Last, the ghosts of the static walls are added where a piece uncovered their band (lattice points outside a static wall, within the band of the interior, state of the nearest ghost of the same tag). mass_residual is the change of interior_mass over the call: the content of what was covered and uncovered. Nothing is renormalised to domain_volume, which is the volume at t = 0. A piece with points on a periodic end is refused (pieces have no periodic images).

Requires valid neighbour lists. Returns the counts and the content change of the call; (0, 0, 0) on a domain without moving pieces.
