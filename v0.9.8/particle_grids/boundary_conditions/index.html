Boundary Conditions

Boundary conditions act on the boundary (ghost) particles of the narrow band around the physical region. They are registered per boundary tag in the domain's bc_map and applied by apply_boundary_conditions!, which iterates over the map and calls each condition's functor.

The time stepper applies them to every state it builds: each stage state $Y_i$ (after the implicit solve) and the final state. MOOD re-forms a candidate after an order drop, and the boundary conditions are re-applied to that candidate as well. Interior particles are never touched by a boundary condition, and boundary particles are never updated by the spatial scheme; their right-hand side is zero. They do take part in the scheme's gather pass, so a boundary particle carries a reconstruction gradient like every other particle and both sides of a wall pair are reconstructed (up to 0.8 the ghost side was piecewise constant, which made the wall rows first order whatever the condition).

On moving grids, boundary particles keep their positions (their grid velocity is zero).

Built-in conditions

On a domain with moving pieces the ghost particles of a piece follow it; the fit-based conditions below evaluate them at the time of the state they build and hand the wall velocity to their operator.

  • FixedDirichlet sets every boundary particle with the given tag back to its value in pg.rhos. Because boundary particles are never advanced, this is their initial state.
  • OutflowBC is a zero-gradient (transmissive) condition using a nearest-donor sweep. Interior particles act as donors; in up to five passes, each unresolved boundary particle with the tag copies the state of its nearest already-resolved neighbour. A boundary particle that finds no donor keeps its initial state.
  • ExtrapolationBC(degree) is the transmissive condition of polynomial degree degree (0–5): the boundary particle takes the value at its own position of a one-sided MLS fit through the interior (see below). Degree 0 is the weighted average of the data (the donor and its interior neighbours), close to the donor copy of OutflowBC; degree q keeps the first q derivatives of the interior solution continuous across the boundary. It needs no wall geometry.
  • MirrorBC(op; degree, bounded) is the wall condition built from the mirror image: the one-sided fit is evaluated at the mirror point $x - 2\varphi n$ behind the wall ($\varphi$ the wall distance, $n$ the outward normal of the particle, from the domain's shape), and the boundary particle stores op(eq, U, wall::WallPoint) of the value U found there. Without op the mirrored value itself is stored, an even reflection of every component (a symmetry plane, zero normal derivative). Physics packages supply the operators of their walls: a slip wall reflects the normal velocity, op(eq, U, w) = reflect_velocity(eq, U, w.normal); a diffuse kinetic wall builds a Maxwellian from the wall state. op must be a bits-type callable (a plain function or struct without captured arrays). With bounded (the default) the mirrored value is clamped to the range of the data of its fit before op is applied, see the stability notes. On a planar wall the mirror image is exact and the condition as accurate as the fit; on a curved wall it is second-order accurate. Requires a shape-based domain.

The fit

ExtrapolationBC and MirrorBC share one kernel. For a boundary particle it

  1. finds the donor: the interior particle nearest to the target point (the particle itself, or its mirror image), searched in the particle's neighbour list, then in its neighbours' lists (the outermost ghosts of the $R + \Delta x$ band have no interior neighbour), and refined by walking the neighbour graph towards the target;
  2. chooses the degree $q$: starting from the requested one, it is lowered one step at a time until the donor and its interior neighbours number at least $1.5\times$ its unknowns, or, failing that, an interior neighbour of the donor does (the one nearest to the target, which then becomes the donor: a corner particle has 3–4 interior neighbours for the 6 unknowns of a 2-D quadratic, its neighbours further inside more, and a well-supported fit a little farther away is still exact for polynomials while a lowered degree is not); then at most the MOOD order of that donor minus one; in 3-D at most 3;
  3. fits a polynomial of degree $q$ centred at the target point through the donor and its interior neighbours only (ghost states are being built and must not enter), weighted by their distance to the target with the grid's weight function, with the basis scaled by the largest data distance (which balances the moment matrix, important in Float32) and with the conditional regularisation of every MLS fit; the constant term is the value at the target. The degree is lowered further if the moment matrix does not factorise; degree 0 is the weighted average of the data.

An operator that declares uses_wall_state gets the same fit's polynomial evaluated at the wall point as a second argument, op(eq, U, wall, U_wall): a balance imposed at the wall (the returning flux of a diffuse kinetic wall) must not be taken at the mirror point, whose depth differs from ghost to ghost (a first-order error of the scheme's flux through the wall out of equilibrium). A boundary particle without any interior particle within two hops keeps its state. The cost is one small fit per boundary particle per stage, a few percent of a flux evaluation (on a 200² grid with 2 700 ghosts, 2 threads: 0.34 ms for ExtrapolationBC(1), 0.53/0.86 ms for MirrorBC degree 1/2, against 0.16 ms for OutflowBC and 7.5 ms for a step; degree 3 with R = 3.5Δx costs 2–3 ms).

Stability

The ghost states feed back into the interior scheme through the stencils of the near-wall particles, so a condition that amplifies perturbations of the interior data grows without bound. Three measures keep the fit-based conditions stable; they were found on a 2-D channel (periodic in x, walls at y = 0 and 1, jittered cloud, R = 2.5Δx) with a Gaussian centred on the wall advected along it for two periods at half the scheme's own time step, with unlimited MUSCL3 and MUSCL5 (the worst case: no limiter, no MOOD):

  1. No ghosts close to the wall. A ghost much less than Δx outside the wall, with the first interior particle up to Δx inside, has its mirror point in the gap between them, so the fit extrapolates, and the ghost is slaved to the interior next to it every stage. Such ghosts arise from a jittered wall row (about half of it crosses the wall) and from every wall off the lattice (curved walls, obstacles, box extents that are not a multiple of Δx). The point generator therefore reflects jittered interior points back inside and snaps points less than Δx/2 outside a wall onto it, as interior particles (Point generation). With those ghosts MirrorBC did not converge: on this channel 0.32/0.32/0.35 at N = 20/40/80 (t = 0.5, MUSCL3), on the same channel with the wall 0.1Δx off the lattice row and no jitter 0.41/0.67/0.63 at t = 1; the unbounded mirror grew with MUSCL5, and OutflowBC lost accuracy. Now the mirror converges with about the error of exact ghost values (with the ghost gradients below, jitter 0.3Δx: 0.27/0.063/0.010, exact ghosts 0.15/0.043/0.0084; off the lattice 0.38/0.12/0.020 at t = 1, exact 0.23/0.074/0.016; before the ghost gradients the mirror gave 0.28/0.062/0.023 and 0.36/0.095/0.025), and MUSCL5 with degree 1 or 2 stays bounded over two periods with and without the bound of item 3.

Ghost gradients (found through the mirror). With ghosts left out of the gather pass (gradient zero), long runs along a wall lost convergence with MirrorBC at fine resolution (N = 80 → 160, t = 1: 3.8e-2 → 2.9e-2, order 0.4, the error growing linearly in time) while exact ghost values converged (1.5e-2 → 7.8e-3), and a condition that only copies the numerical value of the exact mirror particle behaved like the fit, so the fit was not the cause: every wall pair was reconstructed piecewise constant on the ghost side, which made the wall rows first order, and a condition that mirrors the numerical solution feeds that error back. With the ghosts in the gather pass exact ghosts give 1.6e-2 → 2.4e-3 and the mirror 2.0e-2 → 2.6e-3 (order 2.9), on and off the lattice rows. OutflowBC along a wall gains as well (5.7e-2 → 8.9e-3 at N = 160), and the 2-D ExtrapolationBC(2) box that grew before stays bounded.

Since the ghosts are gathered they carry what every particle carries: a reconstruction gradient, the curvatures MOOD's MOODu2 reads, and possibly a lowered order (a starved ghost keeps the highest order its neighbours support; under EPD1 an edge to it runs at that order). MOODu2 compares a particle's curvature with its neighbours'; with the ghosts' curvatures zero its smooth-extremum test always failed on the wall rows, which made it MOODu1 there (a Gaussian on a mirrored channel wall, MUSCL3, MOODu2(1e-4), N = 80: 5.8 order drops per step on the wall rows and error 7.4e-2 before, no drops and 1.0e-2 now). The gather of the ghosts costs up to about 20 % of a step where they are many (3-D MUSCL5 at N = 16, 59 % ghosts: 29 → 35 ms; 2-D a few percent). Leaving out the ghosts that no interior particle reads (13–37 % of the band, depending on the stencil) saves nothing measurable: those are the outer rows, with few neighbours and cheap fits.

  1. The fit is centred at the target point and weighted from it. A Taylor fit centred at the donor and evaluated at the target amplified perturbations on jittered clouds (the mirror point often lies between the donor and the wall, outside the one-sided data cloud) and grew without bound for every degree ≥ 1.
  2. The degree margin (1.5× the unknowns): an exactly determined one-sided fit has large coefficients.
  3. The bound of MirrorBC: the mirrored value is clamped to the range of its data. On clouds that still had ghosts on the wall (item 0) it was what kept every tested case below the initial maximum (MUSCL5 grew by ×2, ×7 and ×180 for degrees 1–3 without it); on the current clouds the bounded and the unbounded mirror give the same result in this test, and the bound stays the default as a safeguard. (Under MUSCL3 the donor-order cap runs a requested degree 3 as degree 2.) OutflowBC itself was not safe in that setting (MUSCL5 at N = 20 grew by ×3.7).

ExtrapolationBC has no bound (an extrapolation legitimately leaves the range of its data) and is an outflow condition: on a 2-D outflow (velocity (1, 0.3) leaving through two faces, bump gone by t ≈ 1, run to t = 2; measured before item 0) degree 1 is as stable as OutflowBC with MUSCL3 and MUSCL5, degree 2 and 3 grow with MUSCL5 (to 10⁹ and 10¹⁸) and degree 2 mildly with MUSCL3 at N = 40. Use degree 1 (the default) unless a run shows otherwise, and never use it at a wall with tangential flow (there even degree 1 grows: use MirrorBC). In 1-D (a bump leaving through the right wall, t = 0.6, MUSCL5) degree 3 converged at 4th order and reduced the error by 10–10⁴× against OutflowBC at R = 3.5Δx, and degree 4 at R ≥ 4.5Δx at 5th order (a degree q needs R ≳ (q + 0.5)Δx in 1-D), so the higher degrees are worth having where the flow is genuinely outgoing. In Float32 use degree ≤ 2 (degree 3 extrapolation amplifies the round-off to 10⁻¹), and in 3-D at most degree 3 is used.

Writing your own

Subtype AbstractBoundaryCondition and implement the functor:

struct MyBC <: AbstractBoundaryCondition end

function (::MyBC)(pg::ParticleGrid, rhos_buffer, tag::Int, ts, eq, t)
    @inbounds for i in 1:pg.meta.N
        if pg.core.is_boundary[i] && pg.core.tags[i] == tag
            rhos_buffer[i] = ...   # write in place
        end
    end
    return nothing
end
ArgumentMeaning
pgThe grid; positions, tags, flags, neighbour lists and the wall geometry (pg.core.normals, pg.core.wall_distances) are available
rhos_bufferThe state vector being built (a stage buffer, the MOOD candidate, or the final state). Modify this one, not pg.rhos.
tagThe boundary tag this call is responsible for
tsThe time stepper (useful for type-based dispatch)
eqThe PDE
tTime of the state being built (stage time, or t + dt)

Register the condition with get_rectangular_domain(...; bc_map = Dict(1 => MyBC())). Use pg.shared buffers for scratch space (as OutflowBC does with pg.shared.int_buffer) rather than allocating. A condition that reads the wall normals should return true from requires_shape, so that a domain without a shape is refused when the grid is built instead of feeding NaNs to the kernel. Most wall conditions need no functor of their own: a MirrorBC with an operator on the mirrored state and the WallPoint covers reflections of any kind.

Reference

HyperCloudCore.ExtrapolationBC — Type
ExtrapolationBC(degree = 1)

Transmissive condition of polynomial degree degree (0–5; in 3-D at most 3 is used): every boundary particle with the tag takes the value at its own position of an MLS fit through the interior. The data of the fit are the interior particle nearest to the boundary particle (the donor, found in its neighbour list and refined by walking the neighbour graph) and the donor's interior neighbours; the fit is centred at the boundary particle and weighted by the distance to it with the grid's weight function. It is exact for polynomials up to degree, so an outflow of degree q keeps the first q derivatives of the interior solution across the boundary, where OutflowBC (a donor copy) kinks it; degree 0 is the weighted average of the data.

The degree is lowered where it is not supported: to the order of the donor particle minus one (MOOD lowered it), to what the data count carries (1.5× the unknowns, counting the constant term; a donor at a corner with too few interior neighbours is replaced by its best-supported interior neighbour nearest to the target first), and whenever the one-sided moment matrix is not positive definite even after the conditional regularisation. A boundary particle without any interior particle within two hops keeps its state. The condition needs no wall normals, so it works on every domain.

In Float32, degrees ≥ 3 lose accuracy at the ghosts farthest from the data (a cubic evaluated up to R + 4Δx beyond one-sided data amplifies the round-off: 2-D, degree 3, jittered: 1e-1 relative error, against 1e-3 for degree 2); use Float64 for them. A degree q needs enough interior data within the cutoff: in 1-D R ≳ (q + 0.5)Δx (degree 4 runs as degree 3 at R = 3.5Δx).

Extrapolation is an outflow condition: the ghost states feed back into the interior scheme through the stencils of the near-wall particles, and a high-degree one-sided extrapolation amplifies perturbations. At a wall with tangential flow use MirrorBC (whose target point lies inside the cloud); see its docstring and the docs for the degrees that were found stable.

A strict, unchanging boundary condition strategy.

Callable / Functor

Enforces a static Dirichlet condition upon boundary particles. Iterates over the grid and resets the state of any boundary particle matching the specified tag to the state stored in reference (pg.rhos, or the state array the time stepper advances) at the start of the step. Nothing else writes the state of a boundary particle (the stage predictor keeps it), so that is its initial state unless the user changes pg.rhos.

HyperCloudCore.MirrorBC — Type
MirrorBC(op = nothing; degree = 1, bounded = true)

Wall condition built from the mirror image: every boundary particle with the tag builds the interior fit of ExtrapolationBC (same donor, data, degree rule and fallbacks) centred at its mirror point x - 2 φ n behind the wall (φ its wall distance, n the outward normal; both from the domain's shape, see wall_geometry) and stores op(eq, U, wall::WallPoint) of the value U found there (or op(eq, U, wall, U_wall) with the same fit's value at the wall point, see uses_wall_state). The mirror point lies inside the cloud, so the fit interpolates. Without op the mirrored value itself is used, i.e. an even reflection of every component (a symmetry plane, zero normal derivative). Physics packages supply the operators of their walls (reflecting the normal velocity for a slip wall, a Maxwellian for a diffuse kinetic wall, ...); op must be a bits-type callable, and the state it returns is used as it is.

With bounded (the default) the mirrored value is clamped, component by component, to the range of the data of its fit (the donor and its interior neighbours) before op is applied: a discrete maximum principle on the ghost state. It costs accuracy only where the value at the mirror point would lie outside the range of the interior data around it (an extremum between the data points), and it is what keeps long runs along a wall bounded: the ghost states feed back into the interior through the stencils of the near-wall particles, and without the bound that loop grew without limit for degrees ≥ 2 with a 5th-order scheme on jittered clouds (and slowly for degree 1), while every bounded case stayed below the initial maximum. bounded = false gives the plain fit (exact for polynomials up to degree at every ghost).

For a planar wall the mirror point is the exact reflection, so the condition is as accurate as the fit; on a curved wall the mirror image is second-order accurate. Behind a convex corner or edge (outside two faces) a ghost is reflected in the face whose tag it carries, the one it lies farther outside of: its stored normal is that face's and its distance is measured along it, so the mirror point lies behind the other face (outside the region; the fit extrapolates the interior there, exactly for linear data) and op sees that face's normal. (The point reflection through the corner, with the diagonal normal to it, gave op a normal that is right for neither face: a slip wall turned the velocity along the wall into the wall at the corner with an inflow, and a uniform flow did not survive the corner.) Requires a shape-based domain (requires_shape).

HyperCloudCore.OutflowBC — Type
OutflowBC <: AbstractBoundaryCondition
(::OutflowBC)(pg::ParticleGrid, rhos_buffer, tag::Int, ts, eq, t, reference = pg.rhos)

A zero-gradient, transmissive boundary condition strategy.

Callable / Functor

Enforces a zero-gradient outflow utilizing a multi-pass nearest-donor algorithm.

  • Initiates all active interior particles as valid state donors.
  • Executes up to 5 symmetrical passes outward, dynamically locating the nearest resolved neighbor using squared distances and copying its state to the target particle.
  • Safely falls back to the state in reference (pg.rhos, or the state array the time stepper advances) at the start of the step for any completely orphaned boundary particles that fail to resolve a donor.

HyperCloudCore.OutflowBC — Method
(::OutflowBC)(pg::ParticleGrid, rhos_buffer, tag::Int, ts, eq, t)

Enforces a zero-gradient outflow boundary condition using a multi-pass nearest-donor algorithm.

HyperCloudCore.WallPoint — Type
WallPoint{D, T, L}

The wall as seen from a ghost particle, handed to the operator of a MirrorBC: position (the wall point the ghost is reflected in), the outward unit normal there, the distance of the ghost to the wall, the wall velocity and acceleration (those of a Moving piece at the wall point, wall_velocity and wall_acceleration; zero for a static wall), the piece's angular velocity as the skew matrix spin (Ω: the wall velocity is w(x) = v + Ω (x - c); zero for a static wall) and the hessian of the region's signed distance at the wall point (the wall's curvature: t ⋅ hessian * t for a unit tangent t is negative where the wall is convex seen from the region, -1/r around a disc obstacle, and positive where it is concave, +1/r inside a circle; zero for a flat wall).

An operator that mirrors the pressure of a fluid at the wall needs the normal momentum balance there, which the even mirror (∂p/∂n = 0) misses at first order at accelerating, rotating or curved walls. Following a fluid particle along the wall, (u - w) ⋅ n = 0 gives ∂p/∂n = -ρ (n ⋅ a + 2 n ⋅ Ω (u - w) - u_r ⋅ hessian * u_r) with u_r the tangential velocity relative to the wall (for a disc spinning in a gas at rest the three terms cancel). The four- and five-argument constructors leave the acceleration, spin and curvature zero.

HyperCloudCore.requires_shape — Method
requires_shape(bc::AbstractBoundaryCondition) -> Bool

true if the condition reads the wall normals and distances of the particles, which only a domain built from an AbstractShape provides. The ParticleGrid constructor refuses such a condition on a domain without a shape. Defaults to false; MirrorBC returns true.

HyperCloudCore.uses_wall_state — Method
uses_wall_state(op) -> Bool

Whether a wall operator of MirrorBC takes the fit's value at the wall point (the foot of the ghost's normal) as a fourth argument: op(eq, U, wall, U_wall), with U the fitted value at the ghost's mirror point. A balance imposed at the wall belongs at the wall point, a diffuse kinetic wall's returning flux for one: taken at the mirror point, it differs from ghost to ghost by the depth of the mirror point (O(Δx ∂f)), a first-order error of the scheme's flux through the wall out of equilibrium. U_wall is clamped to the donor's data range like U when the condition is bounded. In the block pipeline such an operator's reduction gets the wall-point states of all blocks as a further scratch array, block_wall_reduce(op, eq, Y, N, nb, g, wall, Yw). Default false: op(eq, U, wall).
